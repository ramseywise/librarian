## Foundations

- [[Batch Normalization]] — Batch Normalization (Ioffe & Szegedy, 2015) normalizes layer inputs using mini-batch statistics during training to reduce internal covariate shift — enables higher learning rates, reduces sensitivity to initialization, and acts as a regularizer. Rethinking BatchNorm (Wu & Johnson, 2021) exposes pitfalls with EMA population statistics, train/inference inconsistency, and domain shift.
- [[Bradley-Terry Preference Model]] — The pairwise-comparison model that converts human choices between two responses into a scalar reward — the shared formal core underneath RLHF reward models, DPO, and RLAIF preference models.
- [[Constitutional AI and RLAIF]] — Anthropic's two-phase replacement for human preference labels — critique-and-revise SL-CAI followed by RLAIF against a written constitution — plus what AI feedback provably matches and where it still underperforms humans.
- [[Data Engineering Foundations]] — The six stages of data engineering as a pipeline discipline — ingest, transform, orchestrate, warehouse, monitor, feature-serve — where each stage's job is to prepare data for the next, plus the modern-stack tools (DuckDB, Polars, Iceberg, Dagster) missing from Zoomcamp-era material.
- [[Data Science Curriculum Layers]] — The tree-shaped ML curriculum — statistical foundations, then supervised learning branching to model evaluation and independently to unsupervised/ensembles/Bayesian — plus the six-layer analytics progression that precedes it and the branching decision that ends it.
- [[Dialogue Transformers — TED Policy]] — Transformer Embedding Dialogue (TED) policy (Rasa, 2020) applies self-attention at the discourse level — over dialogue turns rather than tokens — outperforming LSTM-based policies on sub-dialogue handling while being simpler and faster than REDP.
- [[DIET Architecture]] — Dual Intent and Entity Transformer (Rasa, 2020) — a multi-task NLU architecture for joint intent classification and entity recognition that outperforms fine-tuned BERT while being 6x faster to train, using plug-and-play pre-trained embeddings with sparse features.
- [[Dilated Causal Convolutions]] — Convolutions with exponentially increasing dilation factors that preserve temporal causality while growing the receptive field exponentially with depth — the key architectural innovation in WaveNet for modeling long-range audio dependencies efficiently.
- [[Git Branch Triage]] — Deciding what to do with in-flight work before switching context — the branch health check that separates merged from unmerged commits, the WIP-branch-versus-stash choice, and reading a stash diff before trusting it.
- [[HDBSCAN with KMeans Fallback]] — Clustering selection strategy that tries density-based HDBSCAN first and falls back to KMeans when silhouette drops below 0.25 — plus the diagnostic discipline that treats a fallback as a feature-quality signal rather than a resolved choice.
- [[Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning]] — MARL, the non-stationarity problem it exists to solve, CTDE as the dominant answer, the value-decomposition and central-critic algorithm families, and the five challenges — including the ~10–20 agent scalability wall.
- [[Neural Probabilistic Language Model]] — Bengio et al. (2003) introduced the idea of learning distributed word representations (embeddings) jointly with a neural network language model — fighting the curse of dimensionality by mapping words to a continuous vector space where semantically similar words have nearby representations.
- [[Notebook Dependency Staleness]] — Migration maps for the three library breaks that strand old ML notebooks — sklearn 0.20→1.4, TensorFlow 1.x→2.x, PyMC3→PyMC 5 — plus the two-phase triage that distinguishes a mechanical import swap from a genuine rewrite.
- [[Open-Domain Dialogue Systems]] — Survey of frameworks for open-domain conversation — retrieval-based (score candidates), generation-based (seq2seq/PLM), and hybrid methods — with two key goals (informative via knowledge grounding, controllable via persona/strategy/safety).
- [[Positional Encoding]] — Sinusoidal or learned position signals injected into Transformer input embeddings — required because self-attention is permutation-invariant and has no inherent notion of sequence order.
- [[Preference Optimization Algorithms]] — The PPO → DPO → GRPO → KTO/IPO/ORPO family — what each removes from the stage before it, the five-algorithm decision table, and why the field shifted from choosing an algorithm to designing a reward structure.
- [[RLHF Pipeline]] — The three-stage InstructGPT pipeline — SFT, then a Bradley-Terry reward model on ~33k comparisons, then PPO with a KL penalty against the SFT policy — and why all three stages are load-bearing.
- [[Reinforcement Learning Foundations]] — The MDP tuple (S, A, P, R, γ), the Markov property that makes RL tractable, the four algorithm families, and the exploration/exploitation tension — with how each maps onto LLM training and agentic tool use.
- [[Reward Hacking and Overoptimization]] — The three failure modes of optimizing against a learned reward proxy — reward hacking, the inverse-U overoptimization curve against KL distance, and the ~15% alignment tax on academic NLP benchmarks.
- [[Self-Attention Mechanism]] — Self-attention (intra-attention) relates different positions of a single sequence to compute a representation — the core primitive enabling Transformers to model long-range dependencies in O(1) path length.
- [[Transformer Architecture]] — The Transformer model architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017) — encoder-decoder stacks of self-attention and feed-forward layers that replaced RNNs/CNNs for sequence transduction, enabling parallelized training and constant-length dependency paths.
- [[TypeScript any Escapes]] — The three ways out of an `any` — a real type, `unknown` with narrowing, or a generic — plus why types files are the highest-leverage place to fix them and how exhaustive-deps catches stale closures rather than style violations.
- [[WaveNet — Autoregressive Audio Generation]] — WaveNet (van den Oord et al., 2016) is a deep autoregressive generative model for raw audio waveforms using dilated causal convolutions — achieved state-of-the-art TTS naturalness (MOS >4.0) and demonstrated multi-speaker conditioning, music generation, and speech recognition from raw audio.
- [[Change the Channel, Not the Count]] — when per-source yield is an order of magnitude below target, mix channels by job and record rights at the source level on three default-deny axes
- [[ML Frame Sourcing — License, Horizon, Prevalence]] — decision: public frames chosen on redistribution license, a time column for temporal split, and prevalence low enough to exercise the sufficiency guard; imbalance ≠ insufficiency
- [[Data Sufficiency Refusal]] — pure never-raising guard on minimum positives per fold that withholds cost metrics rather than caveating them; refuse-not-abort exits 0
- [[Profile Then Select]] — ml-transform profiles every column with stated thresholds and never applies; ml-select refuses to pick before the profile exists, parks unknowns with triggers, emits a draft when nobody can answer
- [[Tabular ML Leakage Taxonomy]] — Six leakage classes for tabular ML — target, temporal, group, fit-before-split, resampling outside the fold, train/serve skew — all answering one question ("at the moment this line runs, what does it know that it would not know at prediction time?"), plus why a leakage verdict needs the person who knows how the data was collected, not a correlation ranking.
- [[Three-Level Duplicate Classification]] — Multi-source entity resolution as not/possible/confirmed duplicate from deterministic, entity-type-specific signals; the uncertain middle goes to review, confirmed duplicates hide via the existing gate, nothing auto-merges
- [[Open POI Data Sources]] — Measured comparison of open point-of-interest sources for a city venue catalogue — OSM tags, Wikidata, Wikivoyage listings, Foursquare OS Places and Overture Maps — covering licence, channel, per-field fill rate, freshness and bias, with the conclusion that open POI data is a triage and proposal signal behind a human gate, never a write path.
- [[Outcome vs Process Reward Models]] — ORM scores the whole rollout, PRM each step; 2025 practice swung back to ORM plus verifiable rewards, with a light PRM for verification
- [[RL Training Frameworks for LLMs]] — verl vs OpenRLHF vs TRL by scale ceiling (70B+ / ~30B / ~13B) and algorithm support, with a selection matrix
- [[Repo Weight Triage — GC Before LFS]] — measure .git layers first; gc, dedupe, untrack media, then gitignore+manifest; against submodules and LFS at personal scale
- [[Calibrated Score, Deterministic Decision Rule]] — A ranked score is not a decision: deterministic code turns a calibrated probability into an action via a cost-based threshold, so calibration is correctness and target definition (ability vs propensity vs uplift) precedes model choice.
- [[Clustering Validation Against Named Baselines]] — validate unsupervised segments against named trivial baselines, leakage analogues and a matrix-health rule that closes the weak-result escape hatch
- [[ML Stage Layering with Typed Contracts]] — organise ML code by process stage with a Pydantic schema per boundary; technique folders become libraries the stages call
- [[Label Horizon vs Sampling Window]] — label definition, label horizon, and sampling window are distinct; a fraud frame's two-day Time span is not a horizon, and applying a horizon standard to one run but not its sibling is the finding
- [[ML Run Reproducibility Bar]] — five-element bar (tests, synthetic-twin dry run, owned guards, run record + reproduce, data sweep) making an ML finding refutable from the record
- [[Training-Agent Disaggregation]] — Make an existing agent trainable without rewriting it: trace runs as state/action/reward into an external trainer, bind trainable roles to logical model names a proxy re-routes, and train each role with the algorithm that fits (SFT, PPO, contextual bandit).
- [[Post-Training for Agents]] — How SFT, RL and distillation divide the work of training an agent — SFT writes form and protocol, RL learns transferable strategy, on-policy distillation gives RL's on-policy data with SFT's dense signal — and why the environment, data and reward shape matter more than the choice of algorithm.
- [[RLVP — Verifiable Path Penalties]] — Add deterministic per-action penalties (and matching compliant-action bonuses) to an outcome reward, R = O + β·Φ, so agent RL learns outcome-neutral constraints like 'don't edit the tests' and recovers the within-group variance GRPO needs — with four design rules that avoid the inaction trap.
- [[DAPO]] — Four changes to PPO/GRPO-style RL — Clip-Higher, token-level loss, dynamic sampling and overlong reward shaping — each aimed at stopping the policy from collapsing early onto a single strategy; the reported state-of-the-art recipe for verifiable-reward math RL.
- [[Rejection Sampling Fine-Tuning]] — Sample several candidates per prompt, keep only those a rule, test or reference answer verifies, deduplicate and cap per prompt, SFT on the survivors, and repeat as the model improves — the STaR/RFT loop that sits between SFT and RL with no reward model and no policy gradient.
- [[On-Policy Distillation]] — The student generates its own trajectories and a teacher scores every token by its full next-token distribution (minimise KL) — RL's on-policy data with SFT's dense signal, matching RL on math in about a tenth of the steps; self-distillation (OPSD) gets the same signal from the model itself given privileged context.
- [[Agent Fundamentals]] — Agent = LLM + Context + Tools, read as policy, observation space and action space — the five-part context and what breaks when each part is removed, the four-step tool call, why RL-trained 'model-as-agent' still needs a harness, the three places an agent can learn, and when a workflow beats an autonomous agent.
- [[Constitutional Classifiers]] — Reference design for classifier guardrails — a natural-language constitution generates the synthetic training data, question and answer are judged together so coded requests are caught, and a near-free activation probe screens everything and escalates only suspicious traffic to a stronger classifier.
- [[Real-Time Multimodal Agents]] — Voice dialogue, computer use and robot control are the three settings where continuous input and a strict output time budget make multimodality hard — and all three are moving the same way, from serial pipelines toward end-to-end models and a fast/slow division of labour, with irreversible actions needing a gate.
- [[Voice Agent Paradigms]] — Cascaded (VAD → ASR → LLM → TTS), end-to-end omnimodal, and full-duplex voice agents differ in one thing — how far each gets from VAD's assumption that conversation happens in discrete turns — and all three coexist as trade-offs; the cascade's serial latency budget is about 1–2 s before queueing multiplies it.
- [[Semantic Turn Detection]] — Replacing VAD's silence threshold with streaming perception that judges end-of-turn from meaning plus acoustics — why VAD + segmented ASR fails on latency, information and accuracy, the two streaming paths, and the label-leakage bug where the fix was causal re-labelling, not a new architecture.
- [[Fast-Slow Thinking Architecture]] — Three ways to give a real-time agent deep reasoning without blocking it — fast fillers then slow answers, a fast interaction model advised by a background reasoner (the 2026 mainstream), or one model that thinks while speaking — plus the text-vs-latent channel between them and the rule that the fast side never claims 'done' before the slow side confirms.
- [[Computer Use Visual Grounding]] — How a GUI agent turns a screenshot into a click — Set-of-Mark labelling, structured DOM/accessibility indexing (prefer it whenever structure exists) and direct coordinate prediction with aspect-ratio-matched rescaling — plus the observation-interface fix for dynamic screens and why per-step efficiency is now the bottleneck.
- [[Action Chunking]] — A vision-language-action policy infers at 1–10 Hz while robot control runs at 50–1000 Hz, so it emits 25–50 actions per inference and replays them while the next chunk computes — smoother motion bought with blindness to sudden change; plus the discrete-token vs. flow-matching action representations and the planner + VLA split.
- [[Programming Curriculum]] — Course map for the three languages an AI or data engineer writes daily — Python, SQL, TypeScript — with the phase order, the hand-offs between tracks, the rules of thumb each track ends on, and where each topic's wiki page lives.
- [[Python Closures and Decorators]] — Functions are values: closures capture variables (not values) from their defining scope, a decorator is a function that wraps a function (always with `functools.wraps`), and a decorator with arguments adds one more level — the shape behind timing, retry and caching wrappers.
- [[Python Concurrency Models]] — Pick by what you're waiting on: threads (ThreadPoolExecutor) for a few dozen blocking I/O calls, asyncio for hundreds of concurrent requests or async SDKs, processes for CPU-bound pure Python — and in asyncio, never block the event loop, bound fan-out with a Semaphore, add deadlines, and retry only transient errors with backoff plus jitter.
- [[Python Data Model and Idioms]] — Names are labels on objects, not boxes — which explains aliasing, the mutable-default trap and pass-by-assignment; dunder methods map syntax onto your classes; hashability rules decide what can be a key; and a short list of idioms (unpacking, EAFP, dict merge) should be reflexive.
- [[Python Exceptions and Context Managers]] — Catch only what you can handle, chain re-raised errors with `raise … from err`, give each package one exception base class with fields callers can branch on, and put cleanup in `with`/`finally` — `@contextmanager` turns a try/finally around a `yield` into a reusable resource guard.
- [[Python Foundations Reference]] — Compact Python-for-Everybody-level reference — types and operators with their traps (floor division, truthiness, short-circuit guards), return-don't-print functions, loop patterns and their built-ins, strings and files, collections and aliasing, regex, HTTP, JSON/XML APIs, classes and sqlite3 — plus the course's rules of thumb.
- [[Python Generators and Lazy Iteration]] — Iterators are single-use and generators run only on demand, so chained generator pipelines process arbitrarily large inputs in constant memory — but a second pass over an exhausted iterator silently yields nothing, and a generator's open `with` block lives until it is consumed.
- [[Python Packaging and Tooling]] — A modern Python project is a `pyproject.toml`, a `src/` layout and `tests/`, run with uv (envs, deps, lockfile), ruff (lint + format) and pytest (plain asserts, parametrize, fixtures) — and most import bugs come from `sys.path` and stdlib-shadowing filenames, fixed by installing the project rather than editing paths.
- [[Python Type Hints and Dataclasses]] — Type hints are checked by tools, not Python: type every signature (loose in, specific out, `X | None` to force None handling, `object` over `Any`), use Protocols for checker-understood duck typing, frozen dataclasses for trusted internal data, and Pydantic to validate untrusted data at every boundary.
- [[Runtime Validation with Zod]] — Types are erased, so env vars, JSON bodies, files, tool inputs and LLM output are really `unknown`: parse them once at the boundary with a zod schema that yields both the runtime check and the static type (`z.infer`), then trust the typed value everywhere after — parse, don't validate.
- [[SQL from Python]] — SQL does the set operations and Python orchestrates: sqlite3 connection/cursor mechanics (the `with` block commits but doesn't close), parameters instead of f-strings with allow-lists for identifiers, DuckDB for SQL over files and DataFrames, and filter-in-SQL/reshape-in-pandas as the division of labour.
- [[SQL Indexes and Query Plans]] — A B-tree index turns a full scan into a log-n lookup but every write pays for it; read EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN for SCAN vs SEARCH, order composite indexes equality-first/range-last, keep predicates sargable (no function on the indexed column), and know when indexes don't help — including columnar engines that use zone maps instead.
- [[SQL Join Patterns]] — Choosing inner, left, self, anti and cross joins by what should happen to unmatched rows — and the two silent-wrong-answer traps: a WHERE filter on a LEFT JOIN's right table turning it back into an inner join, and fan-out when the join key isn't unique.
- [[SQL NULL and Three-Valued Logic]] — NULL means unknown, so any comparison with it is unknown and WHERE drops it — which silently breaks `!=` filters, `= NULL`, and `NOT IN` over a nullable subquery; plus the related expression traps: half-open date ranges, integer division, and dialect-specific string/date functions.
- [[SQL Query Fundamentals]] — Core SELECT/WHERE/ORDER BY/CASE/LIMIT and GROUP BY/HAVING mechanics, organised around the logical evaluation order (FROM → WHERE → GROUP BY → HAVING → SELECT → ORDER BY → LIMIT) that explains most alias and filtering errors, plus the rules of thumb that prevent silent wrong answers.
- [[SQL Schema Design and DDL]] — A schema makes promises about its data and constraints let the database keep them: the four data-hygiene rules behind normal forms, PRIMARY KEY/UNIQUE/CHECK/FOREIGN KEY semantics (and SQLite's off-by-default foreign keys), safe UPDATE/DELETE, ON CONFLICT upserts for idempotent loads, atomic transactions, and views vs materialised views.
- [[SQL Subqueries and CTEs]] — A query is a table, so it can go anywhere SQL expects a table or value: scalar, correlated and derived-table subqueries, EXISTS vs IN (and why NOT IN breaks on NULL), CTEs for readable multi-step queries, and recursive CTEs for hierarchies and gap-free date series.
- [[SQL Window Functions]] — A window function computes over a group but keeps every row — the tool for top-N per group, dedup-latest, running totals, moving averages, lag/lead comparisons and share-of-total; it runs after GROUP BY so it must be filtered from an outer CTE, and its default RANGE frame silently groups ties.
- [[TypeScript Async Patterns]] — A JS promise starts running when it is created (unlike an asyncio coroutine), so the choice is sequential vs Promise.all parallel; Promise.race does not cancel the loser, forEach does not await, and an unbounded Promise.all over thousands of requests needs a concurrency limit — pass an AbortSignal down to cancel real work.
- [[TypeScript Discriminated Unions and Narrowing]] — Model 'one of these' as a union with a literal `kind` field, narrow by control flow, and end the `switch` with `const x: never = s` so adding a member without handling it is a compile error; type predicates package a check but are trusted, not verified.
- [[TypeScript for AI Apps]] — Treat an LLM API as an untyped, slow, occasionally failing remote call: narrow content blocks by `type` (never assume `content[0]` is text), drive the tool loop from an exhaustive `stop_reason` switch, stream long requests, validate tool inputs and structured output with zod, and keep parsing and loop logic pure so it tests offline.
- [[TypeScript for Python Developers]] — TypeScript is JavaScript with a checker that runs and then disappears, so learn the runtime traps first (`==` coercion, string-comparing `sort()`, truthy `[]`, reference equality), then annotate boundaries, prefer `unknown` over `any`, and turn on `strict` from day one.
- [[TypeScript Generics and Type Transformations]] — Generics keep the caller's type through a function (a type parameter should appear at least twice); keyof/T[K], overloads, utility, mapped, conditional and template-literal types derive new types from one source — and type-level code is real code, so stop before infer goes three levels deep.
- [[TypeScript Modules and Tooling]] — Most 'TypeScript is hard' pain is module and config pain: use ESM with named exports and explicit `import type`, one strict tsconfig (nodenext, verbatimModuleSyntax, erasableSyntaxOnly, noEmit), run with Node type stripping and check with tsc separately, and use .d.ts files and module augmentation for untyped or extended libraries.

## Patterns

- [[ACI (Agent-Computer Interface)]] — Tool design discipline for agents — the interface between agent and tools, analogous to HCI for humans. Good ACI determines whether the agent uses tools correctly or hallucinates parameters.
- [[Agent Orchestration Patterns]] — Four levels of agent logic structure — single prompt, single agent + tools, multi-step graph, multi-agent — ordered by control and complexity, with a decision shortcut and the governing rule to start simple and escalate only on demonstrated need.
- [[Agent Quality Review Checklist]] — Nineteen agent-system-specific review checks across six families — prompt/LLM smells, tool safety, workflow state, retrieval/context, memory write-back, and accountability — of which "safeguard in prose only" is named the highest-value finding.
- [[Agent Scaffolding Skill Layers]] — A three-layer Claude Code skill design for scaffolding agents — a generic parallel-subagent factory (L1), standalone capability add-skills that read the target before generating (L2), and a domain-specific bundle that orchestrates L2 skills in sequence (L3).
- [[Agentic Workflow Patterns]] — Anthropic's five composable workflow patterns for LLM agents — when to use each, and the ACI principle for tool design.
- [[AI Project Archetypes]] — Four archetypes — Information Retrieval, Document Generation, Workflow Automation, Conversational Interface — that cover most nonprofit AI projects, each with a complexity floor, disambiguating questions, and a mapping to concrete scaffold parameters.
- [[AI Project Template Scaffold]] — A generic, framework-agnostic starter repo pattern for standing up new AI agent projects — modeled on a mature reference project's skills/docs/infra layout plus a conventional data-science project skeleton (`.github`, `project_init.sh`, `.vscode`, `data/`, `docs/`, `infrastructure/`), kept as its own repo rather than nested under the reference project.
- [[Asked vs Derived Scaffold Variables]] — A scaffold interview that splits ~20 template variables into six asked out loud, eight derived-then-confirmed, and the rest left silently defaulted — with the split decided by blast radius of a wrong guess, not by how many variables exist.
- [[Block Attribute Inversion]] — Turning a list of unanswerable design questions into per-component metadata — when each architectural block ships with its own failure mode, scaling limit, and cost driver, the design's weak points are generated from the assembly rather than interrogated from a user who cannot answer.
- [[Branch Naming Convention Pattern]] — Ticket-linked, type-prefixed branch naming (`type-TICKET-slug`) with per-repo type taxonomies and a `hotfix` escape hatch for production-blocking bugs.
- [[Callable-By Integration Contract]] — When a project needs to work with an external hosted service, the scaffoldable unit is not the service but the contract that makes your system reachable from it — a plain HTTP endpoint plus a signed-webhook receiver for the reverse direction.
- [[Capability Parity Audit]] — A method for deciding what a shared template should absorb next — classify every requested capability as have / partial / gap against the template's *verified* current state, then prioritize by how many consumers share the gap rather than by how loudly any one asked.
- [[Capability Runtime-Coupling Tiers]] — Sorting agent capabilities into runtime-coupled (T1), runtime-adjacent (T2), and runtime-independent (T3) — because cross-runtime parity is a meaningful goal for T1/T2 and a category error for T3.
- [[Chain of Thought]] — Inference-time technique where the LLM is prompted to show its reasoning before answering — improves accuracy on multi-step logic, arithmetic, and routing decisions with no training cost.
- [[Complexity Floor]] — The minimum viable complexity of a project shape — a constraint that makes capacity a selection criterion rather than a schedule, forcing archetype reframing rather than scope-thinning when the team can't reach the floor.
- [[Copier Re-Entry as Capability Path]] — Treating `.copier-answers.yml` as durable scaffold state so that flipping a template answer and re-rendering is the *only* sanctioned way a component enters a generated repo — which turns "scaffold the MVP now, add later" into a real guarantee rather than advice.
- [[Copier Upstream Update Workflow]] — Pulling template changes into an already-scaffolded project — a clean-tree gate, a mandatory `--pretend --diff` preview, impact-categorized change review, and conflict resolution that preserves local intent over template defaults.
- [[Corrective Follow-Up Dispatch]] — Gated subagents that a signal-detection pass failed to trigger get dispatched in a second round when always-on subagents report out-of-dimension signal — treating reviewer observations as a recovery path for missed conditional dispatch.
- [[Data Pipeline Pattern Selection]] — Four ways data reaches an AI system — batch ingest, event-driven, streaming, hybrid — chosen by one question about where the data comes from, with hybrid treated as a phase-2 evolution rather than a phase-1 option.
- [[Deferred Decision Status]] — A three-value status for design decisions — Resolved / Open / Deferred(trigger) — where a deferral must name the concrete event that reopens it, and a triggerless deferral silently degrades to Open so it still blocks the gate.
- [[Derived-and-Hidden Design Decisions]] — A scaffold variable marked `when: false` ships the code correctly and prevents the design conversation entirely — the failure mode where observability and guardrails exist as files nobody chose, distinguished from legitimate derivation by whether a silent default has an irreversible failure mode.
- [[Design-Before-Infrastructure Sequencing]] — The decision to make design scoping a standalone skill that runs before the scaffold interview rather than a wrapper around it — because design and build are separate phases that may be months apart, and merging them makes the combined skill unusable for anyone who already designed.
- [[DESIGN.md Artifact]] — The six-section design record every scaffolded project ships — problem, actors, C4 system context, MVP scope, key decisions, and non-functional constraints — shipped as a placeholder stub when unfilled so the skipped conversation stays visible.
- [[Deterministic Review Substrate]] — Review steps that are mechanical (diff scoping, dedup clustering, schema validation, report rendering) are pushed into a CLI the agent shells out to, so the only work left to LLM judgment is the part that actually needs judgment.
- [[Evidence Classification Model]] — A four-state classification (verified / supported / hypothesis / question) every review finding must carry before it is returned, with self-verification assigned to the producing subagent rather than the orchestrator.
- [[Integration Pattern Selection]] — Five ways an AI system connects to external services — MCP tools, Composio connectors, direct httpx clients, webhook receivers, n8n glue — and the single discriminating question that picks each one.
- [[Merge Impact and Evidence State]] — A two-axis finding schema that separates how much a problem matters (merge_impact) from how sure the reviewer is (evidence_state) — so an uncertain critical finding and a certain trivial one stay distinguishable.
- [[Multi-Agent Role Specialization]] — Multiple agents with distinct roles and tool sets coordinated by an orchestrator — the highest-complexity orchestration, justified only when quality genuinely requires different modes of thinking, and explicitly never a starting point.
- [[Multi-Repo Claude Organization]] — How to organize .claude/, .agents/, and docs/ across related repos — avoiding skill sprawl, maintaining canonical sources, and sharing context between parallel workspaces.
- [[Multi-Step Graph Orchestration]] — A directed graph of processing nodes with explicit transitions, where the LLM executes within nodes but you control flow between them — the pattern for branching, human gates, and retry loops, with a concrete refactor trigger for when to adopt it.
- [[Parallel Dimension Scanner Architecture]] — Code review decomposed into independent single-concern scanner agents dispatched in parallel — each owns one dimension, one ID prefix, and one severity mapping — so review breadth scales by adding agents rather than lengthening one prompt.
- [[Payload Security Defects at Canon]] — When a code-gen payload is about to be mirrored into every scaffolded project, its defects must be fixed in the canonical copy first — a mirror multiplies a single-source bug into every downstream consumer, and the fix window closes once copies exist.
- [[Project Discovery Conversation]] — A guided pre-design conversation that turns a volunteer's pain point into a Project Profile — deliberately withholding all technology vocabulary so the artifact commits to outcomes and constraints, not framework choices.
- [[ReAct Pattern]] — Reasoning + Acting loop — the foundational single-agent pattern where the LLM alternates thought and tool calls until it has enough information to answer. Implemented via create_react_agent in LangGraph.
- [[Read-Only by Default with Explicit Authorization]] — A review agent's safety model built from an enumerated safe-command allowlist plus a single authorization gate placed at the write boundary — so subagents get real shell access for verification while every mutating action, including writing a draft the system itself produced, stops at proposing.
- [[Scope-Gated Reporter Dispatch]] — Sizing a review to the change by declaring a scope up front, then recording each reporter's skip with its reason in the verdict — so "not run" is visible evidence rather than an absent section a reader mistakes for a clean pass.
- [[Scope-POC Design Interview]] — A five-tier system-design interview that produces a DESIGN.md answering *what* to build — idempotent over its own output, ratifying rather than adopting inherited decisions, and treating "I don't know" as a recordable answer instead of a blocker.
- [[Shared Context Brief]] — A structured brief the orchestrator fills in exactly once and passes to every dispatched subagent, so N parallel reviewers share one grounding pass instead of each re-deriving repository context from the same diff.
- [[Silent Fallthrough in String-Keyed Discovery]] — When a tool finds its working target by grepping a literal string, renaming that string doesn't crash — it selects a different target with no error, making the rename a flag-day change whose only defence is a call-site checklist.
- [[Single Agent With Tools]] — One agent with a tool set, where the LLM chooses which tools to call and when to stop — the level most real projects need, and the recommended default before any graph or multi-agent escalation.
- [[Single Prompt Baseline]] — One LLM call with no tools, state, or framework — the simplest orchestration, correct for classification/extraction/summarization, and the baseline any agent must beat to justify its complexity.
- [[Six-Pillar Agent Engineering Assessment]] — A rubric scoring agent codebases across six engineering pillars — prompt, context, harness, loop, graph, evaluation — with each pillar's requirements tiered Must/Should/Nice so a coverage percentage separates "missing the foundation" from "not yet mature.
- [[Skill Preloading via Agent Definition]] — A skill file is inert until an agent definition names it in a `skills:` field — the two-file split (`.claude/skills/<name>/SKILL.md` plus `.claude/agents/<name>.md`) is what turns checklist content into context actually loaded at subagent startup.

- [[Pin Client-Executable Functions With an Allow-List]] — Postgres functions are PUBLIC-executable by default, so assert the exact set of functions anon and authenticated can execute (an allow-list via routine_privileges and results_eq), not \"nothing dangerous\"; trigger-only functions can be revoked because EXECUTE is checked only at create-trigger time.
- [[Keyless Injection Regression Suite]] — Pin prompt-injection defences with three keyless tests: adversarial twin fixtures whose output must equal the clean twin's, schema-omission tests proving model output cannot set privileged fields, and prompt-builder tests proving partner text lands inside a labelled delimiter block.
- [[Advisory Enrichment Breaks Ties Only]] — Server-side lookups against an external registry should be advisory: never fail or delay the request, keep a tri-state (undefined = not attempted, null = no match) plus a stored lookup status, act on the decision only as a tiebreak when user-stated facts are silent, and surface everything else as reviewer flags.
- [[Fix the Generator, Not the Generated Record]] — When review finds gaps in a skill-generated design document, sort findings by where the fix belongs (in the specs but not surfaced = generator bug; tracked; genuinely missing; endorsements), put content in the specs and rules in the skill's template, and validate the skill's checklist by showing it reads zero on a failed output and passes on a reference.
- [[Embeddings That Only Rerank Cannot Recall]] — BM25-only candidate pool makes embeddings rerank-only; RRF alone cannot lift recall
- [[Closed-Vocabulary Parser Silently Drops Invented Types]] — closed type regex silently dropped 481 invented link annotations
- [[Native Library Load-Order Collision]] — LightGBM/torch vendor different libomp builds; first loaded wins, segfault without traceback
- [[Cited-Minus-Read Grounding Check]] — Make a chat agent's grounding checkable: cite pages with a marker that cannot collide with page content, then compute ungrounded = cited − pages successfully read in this answer, emit it as its own event, and never let a cited id drive UI highlighting.
- [[File-Disjoint Agent Waves in One Working Tree]] — Run parallel subagents in one working tree by giving each an exclusive file list per wave, fixing shared interface contracts in a serial Wave 0, adding only optional props, and reserving integration and cross-cutting styling for later single-owner waves.
- [[Render-Scoped Component Types Remount Their Subtree]] — A React component type created inside a render (e.g. a markdown renderer's components map) is a new type every render, so any parent re-render unmounts and remounts the whole subtree, silently detaching observers; hoist the types and memoise the map.
- [[Index Pin Applies Only to Direct Dependencies]] — uv's [tool.uv.sources] index pin reaches only packages the project lists directly, so a CPU-only torch pin is ignored when torch arrives transitively and Linux/Docker builds pull multi-GB CUDA wheels; list the package explicitly in the extra that needs it.
- [[Review Cursor Advances Only Past a Clean Run]] — A scheduled review of what changed since last time keeps a per-repo cursor that moves only after a run completes cleanly, so a failed or partial run re-covers the same range next time instead of silently skipping it.
- [[Per-Column PII Verdicts: Keep, Remove, Move, Change]] — Take PII out of an app schema by classifying every column (Yes / Maybe / ID / No) and giving each one of four verdicts — keep, remove, move to the system of record, or change its shape — with emails replaced by actor ids plus a staff-only view, and free text kept under a retention rule.

## Prompting

- [[Prompt Engineering]] — The pillar hub — prompt ⊂ context ⊂ harness, the "colleague with minimal context" golden rule, and the nine techniques with their authority hierarchy.
- [[Few-Shot Prompting]] — Zero-shot versus few-shot, the three properties good examples need (relevant, diverse, structured) with the failure mode for each, and few-shot CoT.
- [[XML Prompt Structuring]] — Tagging inputs so the model can tell instructions from data — the technique that addresses misattribution, which is the mechanism behind injection.
- [[Structured Output]] — A structured-output prompt is a request, not a guarantee; always pair with harness-side schema validation and prefer constrained decoding where available.
- [[Prompt Templates and Variables]] — The fixed-skeleton/variable split makes prompts versionable, evaluable, and — most importantly — makes the injection boundary explicit.
- [[Long-Context Prompting]] — Longform data at the top (up to 30% quality improvement when the query appears last), XML tags for multi-document inputs, and grounding responses in extracted quotes.
- [[Prompt Chaining]] — Why decomposition beats one large prompt (split attention, contamination), what it costs, and where chains cross into harness territory.
- [[Prompt Injection]] — Instructions and data share one channel with no structural separation — the attack surface, Best-of-N power-law scaling, and defences ordered with least privilege first.
- [[Evidence-Labelled Generation with Deterministic QC]] — model output cites evidence labels E1..En that code verifies against the inputs, so grounding is checked without a judge
- [[Graded-Uncertainty Enrichment Contract]] — three levels of not-knowing (flags, partial, needs_review) so sparse input lowers certainty not quality; `unknown` for enums, empty arrays for multi-selects, partial_but_usable as the anti-backlog route
- [[Capped Tiered Tag Assignment]] — Category defines a primary/secondary/situational tag range; items get 1–3 (default 2) with at most one stretch tag and concrete evidence, because tag inflation kills filtering
- [[Length Limits Are a Fit Layer, Not a Prompt]] — enforce maxLength in characters and truncate in code; cap validate-retry at 2–3 then repair deterministically
- [[Free Text With Deterministic Repair Over SDK-Enforced Schema]] — decision: keep text generation plus a sanitize/validate chain and amend the spec; unmeasured criteria default to the status quo, and enforcement wording must name each path's real mechanism
- [[Prompts as Document Completion]] — An LLM is a document-completion engine, so a prompt is the opening of a document whose most plausible continuation is the answer — five properties (tokens, autoregression, left-to-right attention, truth bias, chat-as-transcript) explain nearly every prompting rule.
- [[Four Prompt Criteria]] — Every prompt must at once resemble training data (the Little Red Riding Hood principle), carry all and only the relevant information (Chekhov's gun), lead to a solution, and have a natural stopping point — chat tuning gives you three for free, never the second.
- [[Prompt Assembly as Snippet Packing]] — The feedforward pass of an LLM app (retrieve → snippetize → score → assemble) ends in a packing problem — pick snippets by priority under a token budget, honouring elastic versions and mutual exclusion, then emit the winners in document order.
- [[SOP-Style Agent System Prompts]] — Write a long agent system prompt as an ordered procedure with per-stage exception branches, not an unordered rule pile — in a Tau-Bench ablation, flattening the same rules into a list cut task success by more than 30% while tone changes barely mattered.
- [[Prompting Technique Selection]] — Which named prompting technique to reach for by problem shape — zero-shot first, few-shot for non-obvious format, CoT and self-consistency for reasoning, ToT for search, PAL for exact computation, ReAct for fresh information, Reflexion when a checker exists, chaining for separable stages.
- [[Self-Consistency]] — Sample several chain-of-thought completions at non-zero temperature and take the majority answer — reasoning paths that converge are more trustworthy than any single greedy path, at N× cost and only when there is a single extractable answer to vote on.
- [[Tree of Thoughts]] — Generalizes chain-of-thought into search — a propose prompt generates candidate intermediate steps, a value prompt rates each (sure/maybe/impossible), and a code controller runs BFS/DFS with backtracking; GPT-4 went from 4% (CoT) to 74% on Game of 24.
- [[Program-Aided Language Models (PAL)]] — The model writes a program and an interpreter computes the answer — few-shot examples are code with the reasoning in comments, so exact computation (dates, arithmetic) moves out of the token predictor; only safe with a sandbox, and today usually a code-execution tool.
- [[Reflexion]] — Verbal reinforcement learning — an actor attempts, an evaluator scores, a self-reflection step turns the score into natural-language feedback kept in memory for the retry; it works only when you get a do-over and an objective evaluator, and reinforces confident mistakes with a weak one.
- [[Sampling Parameters]] — Temperature, top_p, max_tokens, stop and frequency/presence penalties — tune temperature or top_p and one penalty, never both; temperature 0 is not deterministic; best-of-n wants temperature ≈ sqrt(n)/10; many reasoning endpoints fix or reject these knobs.
- [[Stop Sequences and Completion Anatomy]] — A completion runs preamble → solution → recognizable end → fluffy postscript; push structural boilerplate into the prompt, let reasoning preamble run, kill fluff, and decide up front which recognizable end a server-side stop sequence will cut on.
- [[Logprobs as Confidence Signal]] — Where the API exposes token log-probabilities they are the best confidence instrument — gate on average token probability, read classification probabilities off a first token, give every label a distinct first token to avoid prefix collision, and calibrate per-label offsets on labelled data.
- [[Fine-Tuning as Prompt Compression]] — Fine-tuning is prompt engineering by other means — full fine-tuning teaches facts, LoRA teaches which existing behaviours and priors to use, soft prompting learns a vector-valued prompt; reach for it when the static prompt is long, stable and still not followed, then make prompts look like the fine-tuning documents.
- [[Tool Definitions as Prompts]] — Function calling is still document completion — schemas are rendered into the system message (TypeScript-style in one vendor's format, with some JSON-schema constraints silently dropped), so every name, description and enum is prompt text steering a chain of hierarchical classifiers; validate constraints and gate side effects in code.
- [[Prompting Reasoning Models]] — Reasoning models do chain-of-thought natively, so prompts shift from how to think to what to achieve — state goals and constraints, drop manual CoT (it can degrade instruction-following), escalate effort gradually against an eval, and never treat the visible thinking as a faithful audit trail.
- [[Prompt Optimization]] — Treat the prompt as a versioned artifact searched against a fixed eval set and metric — by hand (cheapest layer first, one change at a time), by an LLM proposer (APE, OPRO, meta-prompting) or by a framework (DSPy, TextGrad); an optimizer without a metric is just a rewriter.
- [[DSPy]] — DSPy replaces hand-written prompts with signatures and modules that an optimizer compiles against a metric and training set — bootstrapping and ordering few-shot demonstrations, searching instruction wordings, or distilling into weights; worth it only with a multi-step pipeline, a scorable metric and labelled data.
- [[Evaluating Prompts]] — Prompt-level evaluation — build the eval before the prompt; match test style to what changes (model, single prompt, composition); climb from a git-diffed example suite to an automated harness by solving sample sourcing and scoring independently; then A/B online with success and guardrail metrics fixed up front.
- [[SOMA Judge Rubric]] — Write LLM-judge rubrics as Specific questions on an Ordinal, level-described scale across Multiple Aspects — never tell the judge it is grading its own work, split Goldilocks questions into separate axes, put the rubric before the content, and treat scores as relative.
- [[Diversity Seeding for Synthetic Data]] — Temperature alone does not make synthetic data diverse — inject the randomness into the prompt by sampling combinations of dimension values (TinyStories), generating hierarchically, varying target audience (phi-1), or few-shot query generation for retrievers (Promptagator); a seeded label is a free label.
- [[Code Generation Prompting]] — Prompt shapes for code — instruction→code, comments→code, signature completion with stop sequences, schema→SQL, explain-code, parseable unit-test prompts and fill-in-the-middle — plus the over-refusal trap of generic safety system prompts; context and verification remain the biggest levers.
- [[Stateful Objects of Discourse]] — Collaboration talks about persistent objects, but plain chat duplicates them into the transcript and makes "it" ambiguous — separate the object (own pane, short name, updated in place) from the conversation, prefer diffs and edit tools over regeneration, support several objects, and feed user edits back as diffs.
- [[Prompt Leaking]] — Extracting a system prompt, its examples or other confidential context — models refuse direct requests but yield to an escalation ladder (tagged echo, odd tool names, a logging tool, encodings, baiting with partial leaks), so the only safe stance is to assume the prompt will leak and keep secrets out of it.

## Context

- [[Context Engineering]] — The pillar hub — the minimization objective, the four levers (Write → Select → Compress → Isolate) applied in order, and the four context types.
- [[Why Context Is Finite]] — Attention divides a fixed budget across n² token pairs, so added tokens thin attention rather than expanding capacity; the marginal return curve turns negative, not merely flat.
- [[Context Anatomy]] — What goes in the window and in what order — system-prompt altitude, the five layers organized by stability, and stable-before-dynamic ordering for cache prefix matching.
- [[Context Retrieval Strategies]] — The shift from pre-computed retrieval to just-in-time context, the hybrid default with the ≥80%-of-turns test, and pre-retrieval pipeline ordering.
- [[Context Compaction]] — Transforming interaction history into a continuation state — retention priority, why tool-result clearing is the highest-value move, and why state extraction is what makes compaction safe.
- [[Memory as Context]] — Memory is the mechanism by which context outlives a window: episodic→semantic distillation, structured note-taking, index-plus-detail, and hygiene rules.
- [[Multi-Agent Context]] — Sub-agent isolation is the highest-cost lever — the discarded window is the feature, orchestrator-holds-plan is the default, and isolation doubles as a security boundary.
- [[Context Failure Modes]] — Five distinct failures (rot, poisoning, distraction, clash, injection) with separate mechanisms and fixes, plus the diagnostic flow.
- [[Compression Strips the Epistemic Frame]] — summarisation keeps headline facts and drops how-sure/how-known/how-changed; keep prose canonical, mark compression boundaries, audit for hedging drift
- [[Measuring Fixed Context Overhead]] — first-turn cache_creation_input_tokens on cold sessions as the fixed-prefix cost; declared files were only ~half of it
- [[Agent Status Bar]] — A short, code-maintained, key-value block the harness appends at the end of the context every turn (counters, time, TODO, environment, errors) — it works because in-context learning is closer to lookup than aggregation, so distilled state beats raw history; it is lossy and trusted almost unconditionally, so its accuracy must be monitored.
- [[Context Window Budgeting]] — Deciding the token split across instructions, evidence and working space before the window fills — reserve output first, cap every growing component, value each chunk by its marginal information, and set the budget by ablation against the effective (not advertised) window.
- [[Cache-Stable Context Layout]] — Once prompt caching carries real money, byte-stability of the prefix becomes an architectural constraint — the usual cache breakers (timestamps, per-request config, reordered tools, sliding windows, per-request few-shot) and the three design moves that keep the key stable (cache-boundary ordering, byte-aligned sub-agents, frozen tool-result replacements).
- [[Reasoning State Pass-Back]] — The server renders API messages into one token stream through a model-specific chat template, so mislabelled roles change behaviour (a tool result sent as a user turn can reset reasoning mid-task), and current reasoning models expect their prior reasoning returned verbatim inside the tool loop — stripping it makes agents repeat ruled-out hypotheses.
- [[Context-Aware Compression]] — Compressing tool results with the query and the agent's known state in the compressor prompt — in a course experiment it finished in 7 iterations and 40k tokens where per-result and combined summaries took 10–12 iterations and 93k–277k, while keeping identifiers verbatim; compression is understanding, so the compressor needs near-main-model capability.
- [[Structured Context and Schemas]] — Structure compresses meaning rather than tokens — tags for boundaries, markdown for prose, JSON for anything code reads back; schemas belong in three places (input, state carried across turns, output), and state should be small, structured, versioned, validated and overwritten rather than appended.
- [[Cognitive Tools]] — Reasoning steps packaged as callable prompts (understand, recall, examine, backtrack), each run in its own isolated context — prompting alone lifted a non-reasoning model on a maths benchmark from 26.7% to 43.3%, best when the model chooses which tool to call; gains shrink on reasoning models, and a deterministic check beats a model verifier wherever one exists.
- [[Protocol Shells]] — Pseudo-code prompt structure (name, intent, input, process, output) from a no-code context-engineering track — the explicit four parts measurably improve consistency, but the syntax itself has no special power; a markdown prompt with the same sections performs about the same.
- [[Evaluating Context]] — Context evals ask whether the right material reached the window and was used — instrument the failure chain (exists → retrieved → survived → used), measure each lever with its own metric, sweep length and position to find the effective window, and set the budget by component ablation; tokens per successful task is a quality metric.
- [[Context System Maturity Ladder]] — Five rungs of context machinery — single instruction, plus examples, plus carried state, plus coordinated specialist contexts, plus reasoning tools and verification — with the rule to climb only when the rung below fails measurably, seven recurring patterns, four reference architectures, and the order of design questions.
- [[Context Field Theory (Speculative)]] — Provisional — the neural-field / attractor / resonance / quantum-semantics vocabulary from part of a context-engineering course is metaphor, not method; only three ideas survive translation (early framing is sticky, salience comes from structure and repetition, ambiguity is resolved by context), and one underlying result (a symbolic abstraction→induction→retrieval circuit) is real.
- [[Index-Time Distillation]] — Naive chunk-and-retrieve fails on aggregation and rule-induction questions however good the retriever, because attention is soft retrieval, not a summariser — spend compute at index time to turn cases into statistics, rules and hierarchy (RAPTOR, GraphRAG, L0/L1/L2 filesystem tiers, factor discovery).

## Loop

- [[Loop Engineering]] — The fourth layer of the stack — designing the cycle that re-prompts, checks, and stops an agent when nobody is watching, replacing yourself as the person who prompts it.
- [[Loop Termination Design]] — Stop rules are layered and independent — success verifier, iteration cap, budget cap, stall detector, escalation path — and the cap is the backstop, never the primary exit.
- [[Loop Autonomy Ladder]] — Four rungs of handoff — tool approval, stop condition, trigger, session — climbed one at a time, each earned with a verifier that has been observed catching a real failure.
- [[Evolve Loop]] — A slow loop pointed at a fast one that rewrites files rather than weights — four edit targets, a 5–10 run cadence, and the anti-busywork rule that makes "no change needed" a first-class success.
- [[Recursive Self-Improvement]] — Level 4 at the frontier — the write boundary is the load-bearing design decision, a 3% hit rate is fine when attempts are cheap, and automating generation shifts the bottleneck onto verification.
- [[Loop Scorekeeper]] — The scorekeeper (verifier/grader) decides whether a loop's cheap repeated motion becomes value or slop — it must be objective, automatic, fast, clear and hard to fake, live outside the loop's write scope, anchor on deterministic checks, and be written before the loop's first pass.
- [[Loop State File]] — A long-running loop must forget on purpose and remember on disk: a small state file in three tiers (now / recent / archive) with five fields — goal, last score, what was tried, what is next, waiting for a human — kept current against context drift, read at the start of every pass of a guarded Ralph loop.
- [[Three-Layer Trajectory Verification]] — Before a self-improving loop learns from a run it must verify it at three layers — outcome (was it done, by code), process (was it done in an allowed way, by code), quality (was it done well, by an item-wise LLM rubric) — and keep the verdict structured, not scalar, so it says what to edit.
- [[Four Media for Agent Lessons]] — A self-improving loop must decide not only which config to edit but which medium holds the lesson — experience doc, prompt/Skill, program/harness, or weights — promoting lessons along that path as evidence builds and always choosing the smallest target that is easiest to validate and roll back.
- [[Self-Modification as a Release Process]] — An agent 'modifying its own code' should never overwrite the running process: each self-edit is a candidate branch with a falsifiable change contract, failure replay and retention suite before canary; safety machinery stays outside its write scope, and the loop is measured stage by stage because harness updating is not harness benefit.
- [[Sleep Learning]] — The offline half of a dual loop: the online loop only appends immutable evidence, and a scheduled consolidation pass (trigger, orient, consolidate, validate, prune and re-index) turns it into long-term knowledge — so one lucky success or injected page can't immediately rewrite behaviour.

## Graph

- [[Graph Engineering]] — The fifth layer of the stack — designing which nodes exist, which transitions are permitted, and how the runtime work graph mutates, so multi-agent work has an organizational structure rather than just more agents.
- [[Graph Topology Primitives]] — Nodes, edges, state, durable execution, and typed edges — the agency budget per node, conditional edges as where control lives, and reducers as the concurrency primitive.
- [[Loop-to-Graph Escalation]] — A loop is a graph with one node and an edge back to itself; escalation is a cost you justify with five specific signals, not a maturity level you graduate to.
- [[Graph Governance and Attribution]] — Once work fans out across nodes, "the graph did it" is not an acceptable audit answer — identity propagation, per-node cost attribution, and approval gates placed where consequence concentrates.
- [[Knowledge Graph Retrieval]] — Vector search finds things that sound like your question; graphs find things that are connected to your answer — but traversal depth compounds error, which makes entity resolution the load-bearing sub-problem.
- [[Knowledge Graph as Shared Agent Memory]] — Loop → swarm → graph as three capacity unlocks: parallel workers rediscover the same findings because nothing connects them, and a typed KG turns fan-out from re-derivation into accumulation.
- [[n8n AI Workflow Builder]] — A shipped supervisor-pattern LangGraph with published operational constants — five specialist subgraphs, prompt-only specialization, per-node iteration bounds, and an agent never allowed to fill its own context with the artifact it is editing.
- [[Multi-Agent Topology Patterns]] — The five recurring graph shapes (generate-then-verify, supervisor, fan-out/fan-in, subgraph, handoff), what each costs and how each fails, plus the two design questions underneath them: is context shared, and who decides what runs next.
- [[Multi-Agent Failure Modes at Scale]] — A single loop fails by not converging; a graph fails mechanically (cycles, deadlocks, races, unbounded fan-out, mis-routing) and through control failures between loops — and swarm-scale faults like correlated errors and Byzantine workers are caught only by independent checks and anchors.
- [[Text-to-Cypher]] — LLM-generated Cypher over a knowledge graph is reliable only as a generate-then-verify loop: an annotated LLM-ready schema, relationships-before-query output ordering, intent detection, a pre-execution EXPLAIN validator and a bounded retry edge — and it belongs last in the retrieval order, after vector search and canned traversals.
- [[Knowledge Graph Fundamentals and Cypher]] — A knowledge graph is an ever-evolving, domain-specific graph of typed entities and named relationships; build it purpose-first from the questions it must answer, choose LPG vs RDF by goal (LPG when edges need provenance), and write Cypher with bounded paths, MERGE-on-identity-key plus uniqueness constraints, and provenance on edges.
- [[Knowledge Graph Entity Resolution]] — Building a KG from text with LLMs: extraction is the easy part — the graph is only as good as entity resolution, so keep raw mentions in a reversible metagraph, resolve with relational evidence (WCC, LLM clustering with descriptions, ontology NED), and gate loads on false-merge rate weighted above false-split.
- [[Graph Machine Learning and GNNs]] — Graph ML predicts the edges and labels a knowledge graph doesn't have — node classification, link prediction, community detection, graph classification — and since nodes aren't i.i.d., domain-informed features often beat generic embeddings; a predicted edge is a claim, written with confidence and kept out of default traversals.

- [[Single-Call LLM Gate Weakness Audit]] — seven defect classes in a one-call LLM publish gate and the baseline → deterministic fixes → measured experiments order
- [[Small-N Reporting Floor]] — every rate carries N; <30 raw counts only, <100 INSUFFICIENT-N banner; deltas only across the same population
- [[Human Verdicts as Judge Fixtures]] — export human right/wrong labels into judge fixtures with the decision in metadata, score agreement on a hash holdout beside the human ceiling, sample disagreements back
- [[Paired Input-Perturbation Eval]] — perturb only the field a skip gate wants to ignore on real rows, diff model outputs, report material vs immaterial change within and across output buckets
- [[Own-Oracle Fixtures and the Blind Relabel Path]] — escape fixtures authored by running the function: export real rows with expected=null and observed separate, human-label from the rule, then gate at the observed rate
- [[Error Analysis (Open and Axial Coding)]] — Find failure modes by reading ~100 traces before choosing any metric — free-text note on the first thing that went wrong (open coding), group notes into 5–15 categories (axial coding), rank by frequency × cost, and build graders only for failures you have actually seen.
- [[Eval Failure Taxonomy]] — A fixed set of stable failure labels, ordered by pipeline stage, one label per fix, including the eval's own failures (context_missing) — the shared vocabulary for graders, dashboards, gate reports and bug tickets.
- [[Judge Calibration (TPR, TNR and Corrected Pass Rate)]] — Calibrate an LLM judge against 100–200 human labels split train/dev/test, report TPR and TNR separately (TNR usually matters most), benchmark against human–human agreement, and correct the observed pass rate with (p̂ + TNR − 1) / (TPR + TNR − 1).
- [[LLM Judge Biases]] — Position, verbosity, self-preference, leniency, format/authority and capability limits — the documented ways LLM judges go wrong, each with a mitigation (both-order pairwise, different model family, binary criteria with tricky-FAIL examples), plus the pairwise-vs-pointwise and Elo/Bradley–Terry choices.
- [[Grader States (Heuristic, Calibrated, Experimental)]] — Give every grader an explicit state — heuristic (deterministic code), calibrated (judge with human-agreement evidence), experimental (new or uncalibrated) — and let only the first two gate a release; behind it sits one grader interface with a requires field and a grader_version.
- [[Veto Rubric Items]] — A must-pass rubric item that zeroes the total when it fires — hallucination is the classic one — inside a hybrid scoring scheme of must-pass gates plus a weighted remainder, so fluency cannot buy off a fabricated fact and keyword stuffing cannot pad past it.
- [[Eval Error Bars and Sample Size]] — Every pass rate is an estimate — SE = √(p(1−p)/n), so on 50 tasks 80% is ±11 points; size the suite from the smallest difference you must detect, average trials within a task before computing SE, cluster SEs for grouped tasks, and correct for multiple comparisons.
- [[Paired Eval Comparison]] — Compare two versions on identical tasks via per-task differences (McNemar for binary outcomes) — only discordant tasks carry information, so pairing shrinks the error bar for free; gate on the CI of the difference, read the flipped transcripts, and treat each fix as a tiered, cost-weighted hypothesis.
- [[Eval Metric Interpretation Pitfalls]] — Ways a correct-looking eval number misleads — flat headlines hiding cancelling slice moves (Simpson's paradox), dropped errored rows shrinking the denominator, ratio metrics whose denominator moves, novelty effects — and the rule: count errors and refusals as failures or report them separately.
- [[Run-Then-Assert Eval CI]] — Split an eval job into a slow, costly RUN phase that writes results.jsonl and a fast deterministic ASSERT phase that computes CIs per slice against a stored baseline — tiered by trigger (smoke per PR, regression on prompt/tool paths, full nightly), with every gate emitting metrics, labels, worst slices and a next action.
- [[Simulated User Evaluation]] — Evaluate multi-turn agents with an LLM playing the user toward a goal — realistic only with progressive information disclosure (known facts plus rules for when to reveal them), optionally dual-control where the user acts on shared state — graded per turn and per conversation, versus canned conversations that cannot see compounding errors.
- [[Eval Contamination Defenses]] — If eval rows reach a model's training data, prompts or index, the score measures memory — public benchmarks fight it with unique multi-source answers, parameterized task templates refilled every run, temporal freshness and canary GUIDs, and each tactic transfers to a private suite.
- [[Ablation Switches and Feature Debt]] — A master switch that turns off an agent product's major features (thinking, compaction, memory, background tasks) gives a bare-model baseline, and per-feature switches answer whether each still helps — run before every release to find feature debt that newer models no longer need; companions are two-layer feature flags, deterministic prompt-rendering snapshots and privacy-typed analytics.
- [[Parameterised Benchmark Instances]] — The answer to benchmark leakage for agent evals — publish the generator, keep instances private and fresh per run, grade on final environment state, and when training on a benchmark reuse environments but hold out whole templates or domains.
