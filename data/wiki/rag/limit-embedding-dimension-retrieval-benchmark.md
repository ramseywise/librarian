# LIMIT Embedding Dimension Retrieval Benchmark

Weller et al. (Google DeepMind and Johns Hopkins, ICLR 2026) argue that a single-vector embedding of dimension d cannot return every possible top-k subset of a corpus. Theorem 1 (unit vectors, dot product, score margin 2*gamma between relevant and other documents) gives n choose k <= (1 + 1/gamma)^d, so d must grow with the log of n choose k. This is a lower bound only; it ignores how gradient learning would find such vectors.

## Mechanism

- With gamma = 0.1 the bound is small for k = 2 (4 at n = 100, 21 at n = 10^11) but grows with k: 905 for k = 100 at n = 10^11 and 8098 for k = 1000. The authors say these already exceed dimensions used in web-scale search, which are often truncated below 1k, while research models top out near 4096.
- A "free embedding" test optimises vectors directly on the test relevance labels (k = 2, full-batch InfoNCE, Adam). The largest n that still reaches 100% accuracy follows a cubic in d (r2 0.999) fitted on d = 4 to 45; extrapolated, d = 4096 gives about 250M documents and d = 512 about 500k.
- **LIMIT** turns this into a dataset: documents are short statements of the form "person likes X", queries ask "who likes X", and every pair of relevant documents is a query. The full set has 50k documents and 1000 queries; LIMIT-small has 46 documents and 1000 queries (the 1035 pairs, C(46,2), belong to the full setting; the extraction is not consistent here).

## Results (self-reported)

Setup: MTEB framework, single-vector models at their highest dimension, k = 2 relevant documents per query.

- Full LIMIT recall@100: Promptriever 18.9, GritLM 12.9, Gemini embedding 10.0, E5-Mistral 8.3, then Qwen3 at 4.8 and Snowflake Arctic at 3.3. BM25 reaches 93.6 and GTE-ModernColBERT 54.8.
- LIMIT-small: BM25 97.8 recall@2; ColBERT 83.5; the best single-vector models between 19 and 54; none of them solve it at recall@20. Performance rises with dimension (E5-Mistral recall@2 7.9 at d = 32, 29.5 at d = 4096).
- Fine-tuning on a LIMIT train split lifts recall@10 only from near zero to at most 2.8, whereas training directly on the test split solves it; the authors read this as evidence that domain shift is not the cause.
- When synonyms replace the shared wording, BM25 recall@2 on LIMIT-small collapses from 97.8 to 10.6; Qwen3 loses 38.9%.
- Gemini 2.5 Pro given all 46 documents and 1000 queries solved all of them in one pass.
- BEIR and LIMIT scores are generally uncorrelated across six models.

## Limits

- Bound covers single-vector retrieval only, assumes a margin chosen as typical (0.1) rather than measured, and says nothing about tolerating some mistakes.
- The free-embedding curve is a polynomial fit over d up to 45 and k = 2, extrapolated to 4096. A sigmoid loss may need fewer dimensions, so learnability is open.
- LIMIT is synthetic, English only, with an artificially dense relevance structure; the synonym set was generated by an LLM.
- Per the extraction notes, retrieval is exhaustive scoring, so the paper shows nothing about ANN index recall loss. Fig 6 model mapping for the synonym variant is partly ambiguous in the extraction notes.
- The paper's own advice is to keep dense first-stage retrieval and add rerankers, multi-vector or sparse models; it offers no fix.
