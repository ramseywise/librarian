# STAIR ToC-Constrained Generation Retrieval

STAIR (STructure Aware Information Retriever) treats retrieval as generation over a document's own structure. The prompt holds the book's full table of contents (up to 14k tokens) plus the query; the model (Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.2 with LoRA) generates one leaf section title, and decoding is constrained to valid leaves. It is compared against DSI, a generative index trained with the same base model but without the table of contents (512-token input). The benchmark, SearchTome, has 18 open textbooks in 6 domains, with questions generated per paragraph by Mixtral 8x7b. Numbers are the paper's (self-reported); baselines other than Mistral are trained or indexed per book.

## Reported results

- **Recall@1 averaged over domains:** STAIR 82.6, DSI 76.9, DPR 68.7 (an untuned embedder), BM25 59.5, zero-shot Mistral 13.8. Recall@3 90.8 against 85.3 for DSI. The paper prints the DSI gap as "+7.4%", which is 5.7 absolute points (82.6 minus 76.9). The difference is significant at p below 0.05 in each of the six domains (randomization test).
- **Invalid outputs:** 0.05% non-leaf predictions for STAIR against 3.25% for DSI.
- In the paper's figures, DSI hallucinates more as examples per leaf shrink while STAIR stays near zero (no values given in the text).

## Limits

- Corpora with a clean global structure only; no comparison with length-based chunking plus reranking, hybrid RAG or RAPTOR, and no answer-quality results. What comes back is a whole section, not a chunk.
- The test queries were generated by an LLM from the very paragraphs used in training, and every book gets its own LoRA fit, so this is not a zero-shot retriever and a changed corpus means retraining.
- The Haiku 4.5 row (45.6 Recall@1) has no described prompting in the extracted text. BM25 and DPR are not fine-tuned.
- Internal inconsistency: the "+7.4%" gain over DSI is an absolute difference of 5.7 points.
