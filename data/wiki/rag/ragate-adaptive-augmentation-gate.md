# RAGate Adaptive Augmentation Gate

RAGate (arXiv 2407.21712, preprint) asks whether a given turn of a conversation needs external knowledge at all. A gate model predicts augment or skip from the dialogue context. Three variants are tried: prompting a language model (RAGate-Prompt), QLoRA fine-tuning (RAGate-PEFT) and a multi-head attention classifier (RAGate-MHA). Responses are generated by a GPT-2 backbone, with knowledge from TF-IDF or BERT-Ranker retrieval, on the KETOD task-oriented dialogue set with annotated knowledge turns.

## Results (self-reported)

- Always augmenting: BLEU 16.08 against 9.38 without retrieval. Confidence, measured on the generated tokens, fell by 10.43% (8.3677 against 9.3425).
- RAGate-MHA augmented 787 of 4,964 test turns, with BLEU 12.14 and confidence down 0.36%.
- Gate quality is modest: best F1 0.4281 over variants, 0.4082 for QLoRA, and 0.1230 for the best prompting setup. Only about 12.1% of turns are positives.

## Limits

- The extraction notes that the confidence measure is described two ways (minimum token probability, lowest logit), and that the Table 4 caption names SGD while the text says KETOD.
- The extraction notes that calling the gated result "comparable" overstates a BLEU gap of about 4 points to the always-augment run.
- One dataset, two retrievers, no human rating of the final responses.
- Gate F1 stays under 0.43 for every variant.
