# Recruiter Screen Playbook

A 15–30 minute call that opens nearly every loop (see [[AI Role Interview Loop Map]]). The
recruiter scores communication and enthusiasm and pattern-matches your story against the
role. Logistics such as comp, location, authorization and timeline can rule you out before
anyone technical spends an hour on you. All of this is interview performance rather than
durable knowledge, so it decays and gets re-rehearsed per loop
([[Durable vs Performative Knowledge Split]]).

Most candidates under-prepare for this round because it seems easy. Budget 30–45 minutes per
company, mostly on the "why us" research. The narrative is built once and reused.

## The 90-second narrative

```
Past:    "I started in [field], focused on [problem type]."
Pivot:   "Over time I moved toward [the through-line]."
Present: "Right now I'm [role], working on [one concrete thing]."
Bridge:  "This role caught my attention because [specific reason]."
```

- The **through-line** is the load-bearing piece. Name the thread that connects the arc.
- End at the bridge and stop. The recruiter will ask follow-ups.
- Say "I", not "we". Time it, record it, and do three rounds before any real screen.

## Project one-liners

`"[Project]: [what it did] + [scale or outcome], using [tech]."` Build three. They give the
recruiter something to write down, and they open the project deep-dive rounds later, so the
work carries over.

## Comp framing

- Research the median and P75 for this role, level and location beforehand. Treat P75 as
  your negotiation floor.
- Give a range and close with a question: *"I'm targeting X–Y based on market data for this
  role — is that in the range you're working with?"* The close asks them to confirm the band
  early.
- Deflect exact-number pressure ("can you share the band?"). Redirect questions about your
  current comp to the market range.
- **The first number you say becomes a ceiling in the recruiter's notes.** Never anchor
  below your floor. "I'm flexible" sounds unprepared.

## Logistics, decided in advance

Decide notice period, real remote or hybrid constraints (requirement, not preference),
work authorization, start date, and other processes in flight. Saying you are in other
loops without naming companies, with a decision window, signals demand and creates urgency.
It is leverage, not rudeness.

## AI-tool fluency (a 2026 probe)

Recruiters are increasingly briefed to ask how you use LLMs and copilots. A good answer is
**concrete**: name the tools and a specific use case, and say where you choose not to use
them. It is also **honest**: don't claim fluency the technical screen will disprove, and
don't undersell daily use. Understating usage is a yellow flag for AI roles.

## "Why us"

Answer with one concrete thing about the company (real product direction, a recent launch,
a different approach from competitors), plus what the role does and why that matches where
you're heading. Answers like "great culture" and "leader in the space" are filler, and a
recruiter who has screened 50 candidates this week hears them immediately. Frame leaving
your current job as moving *toward* something. Negative framing reads as a risk signal.

## Questions to ask

Ask about loop structure, timeline to offer, and who you'd work with most closely. Then ask
the highest-value one: **"Is there anything in my background you'd want me to address
before the next stage?"** It brings early doubts to the surface while you can still answer
them.
