# Hierarchical Story Generation

Fan, Lewis and Dauphin ask whether a two-step model, a prompt followed by a story, can write more fluent and prompt-relevant long stories. They collected **WritingPrompts**: 303,358 stories paired with prompts, from three years of a Reddit forum.

## Mechanism

- A gated convolutional language model generates the prompt; stories are then generated by top-k sampling with k=10.
- The story model is a convolutional seq2seq (fairseq) whose decoder has gated multi-scale self-attention.
- **Fusion** places a second seq2seq model on top of a fixed pretrained seq2seq, modifying cold fusion to combine two seq2seq models.
- Two new metrics are proposed, since BLEU and ROUGE were judged unsuitable for open-ended text: prompt ranking and human triple pairing.

## Results (self-reported)

- Fusion plus self-attention gave the best test perplexity (WritingPrompts test, 255.4M parameters). The combined additions cut perplexity by 9 points relative to Conv seq2seq, and the gated multi-scale attention lowered it step by step in the ablation.
- Human judges preferred hierarchical-fusion stories with generated prompts to stories from a self-attentive GCNN language model (400 pairs, 5 blind judges each).
- Fusion raised human triple-pairing accuracy over the ensemble by 7% (read from text; the extraction says absolute or relative is unclear).
- Copying check (500 stories of 150 words, generated from test prompts): the fusion model's average LCS with training text is 8.9 words; Conv seq2seq scores 10.2 and a KNN baseline 150, which reproduced a whole training story.

## Limits

- Only perplexity and prompt ranking serve as automatic metrics.
- Random sampling gives repetition, tokenisation errors and missing newlines after dialogue.
- Generated prompts are generic next to human ones.
- Stories are judged at only 150 words; temperature, batch size, steps, hardware and seeds are not reported.
- Neither human study gives significance tests or rater agreement.
