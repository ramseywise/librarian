# Dr. Zero Self-Evolving Search Agents

Dr. Zero (Yue and colleagues, Meta Superintelligence Labs and UIUC, arXiv v2 July 2026) trains search agents with no human QA data. One base LLM is copied into two search agents that have only a search tool. The proposer is given a seed document, searches, and writes a question with its answer. The solver attempts that question n times. Code is released by the authors.

## Mechanism

- **Proposer reward.** With k of n solver attempts correct, the difficulty term is (n−k)/(n−1) when 0<k<n and zero otherwise, so it peaks at a single correct attempt and falls linearly after that. A format reward is added, worth up to 0.5 across four checks (think tags, tool use, question tag, answer tag).
- **Hop-relative advantage (HRPO).** Questions sharing a hop count h form a group, and each advantage standardises the reward against that group's mean and variance. Each seed document yields one question, so nothing is sampled in a nested way. Hop count only defines the groups; difficulty comes from the solver pass rate. Updates are strictly on-policy, with no ratio clipping and a KL coefficient of 0. A single global baseline (REINFORCE++) proved unstable when query structures were mixed, the authors report.
- **Solver.** Ordinary GRPO with an exact-match reward against the proposer's synthetic answer.
- **Loop.** 50 proposer steps then 50 solver steps, up to 3 iterations. Group size 5, batch 256, at most 5 turns.
- **Hop mix.** Earlier data-free proposers drifted to simple questions: among 500 generated by each of SQLM* and R-Zero*, 93.4% and 95.6% were single-hop (hop labels from gemini-3-flash-preview). Dr. Zero's default 1/2/3/4-hop ratio of 4:3:2:1 puts 60% of seed prompts at two to four hops.

## Results (self-reported)

Setup: Qwen2.5-3B and 7B-Instruct, exact match with greedy decoding on NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA, HotpotQA, 2WikiMQA, MuSiQue and Bamboogle, using an E5-base retriever over English Wikipedia and the top 3 passages.

- Against supervised [[Search-R1 Reasoning with Search RL]]: average 0.326 vs 0.327 at 3B and 0.372 vs 0.384 at 7B (the 7B figure is after iteration 2). At 3B it leads on the one-hop sets by relative margins of 22.9% (NQ), 6.5% (TriviaQA) and 18.4% (PopQA). At 7B it gets roughly nine-tenths of Search-R1's multi-hop score and wins 2WikiMQA; MuSiQue and Bamboogle stay with Search-R1.
- Against data-free baselines the authors re-implemented with multi-turn search (SQLM*, R-Zero*), 3B averages are 0.233, 0.256 and 0.326: which is a relative gain of 39.9% and 27.3% on average; across just the four multi-hop sets the gain over R-Zero* is 83.3%.
- HRPO vs nested GRPO (3B, one 8xH100 node, per iteration): 6 rollouts per prompt vs 20, 4.16 h vs 10.58 h, 33.28 vs 84.64 GPU-hours, average 0.326 vs 0.320. GRPO was better on the multi-hop sets (Bamboogle 0.272 vs 0.200 for HRPO) and HRPO on one-hop sets (NQ 0.397 vs 0.361).
- Iterations (3B average 0.304, 0.319, 0.326; 7B 0.366, 0.372, 0.360): gains saturate within 2-3 iterations, and the 7B model got worse at iteration 3.
- Ablations at 3B, one iteration (Table 7's setup says 50 steps, while elsewhere an iteration is 50 proposer plus 50 solver steps): full 0.304; no seed document 0.245; no format reward 0.289. Hop ratios 1:1:1:1, 2:1:1:1 and 4:3:2:1 gave 0.314, 0.316 and 0.326 at 3B and 0.368, 0.369 and 0.372 at 7B, so the authors conclude that balance across hop levels matters more than maximising multi-hop share.

## Limits

- Short-form verifiable QA only, scored by exact match on one Wikipedia corpus with a fixed E5 retriever; the authors say the gains are not isolated from reasoning over the retrieved passages. The proposer's search tool uses the same corpus as evaluation.
- Only Qwen2.5 3B and 7B. The main table is a single greedy run; variance appears only in a separate table (run count not stated). There is no parity at 7B.
- Synthetic answers may be wrong or ambiguous; the grounding rules lessen this without removing it.
- At 7B the model levels off or slips by iteration 3, and inconsistent token IDs across multi-turn steps were the most common failure. Entropy collapse and reward hacking are left as future work.
- Ablations use 3B and a single iteration. The authors wrote their own multi-turn versions of the data-free baselines.
- The extraction could not trust best-cell markings in Tables 1 and 3.
