# OpenAssistant OASST1 Conversation Trees

Köpf et al. (NeurIPS 2023 Datasets and Benchmarks) set out to show that a corpus of assistant conversations, entirely open and both written and ranked by people, can be gathered through crowd-sourcing and then used to align open LLMs through SFT and RLHF. Their hypotheses: models tuned on it beat their base models, and ranking replies written by humans, as opposed to replies sampled from a policy, could be why RLHF adds so little over SFT here.

## Construction

A **conversation tree** has the opening prompt as its root. Every node is a message, its children speak in the other role (prompter or assistant), and the path from root to a node is a thread. Volunteers use a web app that grows each tree task by task: they write prompts and replies, and label and rank replies. Rankings from K independent rankers are combined with Tideman's ranked pairs. OASST1, the release, holds 161,443 messages in 35 languages, 461,292 quality ratings, and over 10,000 complete trees from over 13,500 volunteers; of 66,497 trees, 10,968 reached completion. The authors trained SFT models, reward models under a pairwise loss, and PPO policies (via trlx), on Pythia, LLaMA and Falcon, following the recipe in [[RLHF Pipeline]].

## Results

LMEH is an external leaderboard benchmark. All figures come from single runs and are (self-reported).

- Falcon-40B SFT (top1) scores above Falcon-40B base on LMEH (base: 72.29). Pythia-12B SFT fails to improve on its base (60.33), and LLaMA-30B SFT only just passes LLaMA-65B base (67.24).
- Relative to its SFT model, LLaMA-30B RLHF scores 68.03 on LMEH and has the higher Vicuna Elo (SFT: 979). It is lower on OpenAI Evals and HumanEval: SFT 0.52 and 0.20, RLHF 0.51 and 0.15. The authors conclude RLHF wins on some benchmarks and loses on others.
- gpt-3.5-turbo scores HumanEval 0.72, OpenAI Evals 0.87 and Vicuna Elo 1110; the best OASST model reaches HumanEval 0.20.
- The authors conjecture that RLHF's gains over SFT trail InstructGPT's because their reward model learned from human-written replies instead of SFT-model samples.
- Detoxify toxicity scores are higher for deleted messages (3,422 sampled) than retained ones (N=71,359).
- In a self-selected Discord survey of 270 contributors, more than 95% were pleased to have taken part; 89.1% of annotators are male, with median age 26.

## Limits

- The reward model saw human-written replies rather than policy samples, and RLHF improvements are uneven across benchmarks.
- The annotator pool is 89.1% male with a median age of 26, power users are overrepresented, and English (42.8%) and Spanish (31.4%) make up most of the data.
- Content may be unsafe and may still include text generated by ChatGPT; the authors recommend research use only and flag vulnerability to prompt injection. The political compass findings are described as very preliminary.
- Hyperparameters, compute and seeds are missing from the paper (deferred to GitHub); the benchmark scores are single runs on an external leaderboard.
- The contributor survey is a self-selected sample.
