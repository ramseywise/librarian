# s1 Budget Forcing

Muennighoff et al. (2025, preprint, arXiv v3) ask for the simplest route to test-time scaling plus strong reasoning. They assume pretraining has already put the capability in the model, so a small SFT set that is hard, varied and carefully chosen, plus control over thinking length at decoding time, should be enough. **s1K** pairs 1,000 questions with Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking traces; supervised fine-tuning of Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct on it yields s1-32B. **Budget forcing** shortens thinking by emitting the end-of-thinking delimiter early, and lengthens it by blocking that delimiter and writing "Wait" instead.

## Results (self-reported)

- s1-32B with budget forcing: AIME24 56.7% (greedy, single run), above o1-preview; without budget forcing 50.0%. MATH500 93.0%, GPQA Diamond 59.6%.
- Fine-tuning took 26 minutes on 16 H100s. Training on the full 59K pool costs 394 H100 hours, whereas s1K costs 7. With budget forcing at roughly 30,000 thinking tokens, the 59K model reaches 53.3% on AIME24 to s1K's 50.0%, and the 95% paired-bootstrap interval on that gap is wide.
- On AIME24 in the same setup, the alternative 1K selections score 36.7% (random), 26.7% (diverse) and 33.3% (longest).
- Control, scaling and performance metrics (AIME24, 5 runs): budget forcing has 100% control, scaling slope 15 and the best AIME24 performance; rejection sampling (temperature 1, caps of 3500 to 16000 thinking tokens) shows inverse scaling.
- "Wait" was the best extrapolation string; ignoring end-of-thinking twice gives 53.3% against 50.0%. Extrapolation goes from 50% to 57% and flattens at about six "Wait"s, where over-suppression causes repetitive loops.
- Sequential scaling with s1-32B beats majority voting over 2 to 64 samples on the base model (AIME24). REBASE tree search scaled better than both in one figure, but at 512 steps 12 of 30 questions overflowed the context (earlier data version).
- Training on 4096-token sequences, which cut off 74% of samples, gives 30.0% against 50.0% at 32768.
- Claude 3.7 as grader judges 53.6% of s1K traces correct, versus 63.0% for the s1K-1.1 traces (generated by R1). Without budget forcing, s1.1 gets 50.0% on AIME 2025 and s1 gets 26.7%.

## LIMO: 800 examples

Ye et al. (2025, COLM) propose the LIMO hypothesis: if pretraining has encoded a domain's knowledge thoroughly, a few carefully designed demonstrations of cognitive process can draw out complex reasoning. Their pipeline narrows a pool of millions of math questions to 2,125 difficult ones using model solve rates, selects for each the best of several R1-class solutions by a rule-based quality score, retains the top 800 and fine-tunes all parameters of Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct for 15 epochs. Results (self-reported; pass@1 zero-shot CoT, 32,768 output tokens; AIME24 from 4 samples at temperature 0.6):

- AIME24 63.3% from a 16.5% base; MATH500 95.6% (greedy).
- Average over 10 benchmarks 78.1 against 58.3 for OpenThoughts-114k, 32.3 for NuminaMath-100k and 49.9 for the base model, same backbone.
- Training on 400 samples yields 57.5 on AIME24, and on 2k yields 69.6 (MATH500 95.8); returns diminish past 800.
- Applied to Qwen1.5-32B-Chat the same 800 examples reach only 9.2% on AIME24. Over the Qwen2.5-Instruct sizes the range runs from 2.5 (3B) up to 68.3 (72B).

## Limits

- Gains from budget forcing level off and are capped by the context window. AIME24 has only 30 problems and the runs are single and greedy; results also vary with batch size and parallelism, so gaps of 2 to 3 points sit inside the noise.
- Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking was assessed on AIME24 alone, via a web interface. By the grader's count only about half the s1K traces are right. Filtering relied on the same base model that gets fine-tuned. The paper leaves open whether R1-level performance can be had from 1,000 samples. Fig 7's step-conditional model came from an earlier data version, and v3 added s1.1, AIME 2025, LIMO and o3-mini.
- LIMO covers math only and one backbone family; the 2.5 AIME24 score at 3B shows the effect hinges on the base model. Selecting data drew on difficulty signals from stronger R1-distilled teachers, and the minimum sample count is unresolved.
- LIMO runs are single, without seeds, using an unnamed LLM judge, and hardware and optimizer go unreported. The comparison datasets differ in domain and curation, and the paper's reasons for NuminaMath and OpenThoughts doing worse are conjecture. The extraction mentions later work in which scale beat hand curation for general reasoning, something LIMO never tests.
