# Grover and Neural Fake News

Zellers, Holtzman, Rashkin et al. (NeurIPS 2019) study how realistic controllable **neural fake news** can be and how to detect it when the verifier has only a few examples of the adversary's output. They define neural fake news as targeted propaganda generated by a neural LM that mimics the style of real news. Their hypothesis: the models that generate neural disinformation best are also best at discriminating it, since a left-to-right discriminator has the same inductive bias as the generator and so can spot artifacts of sampling.

## Grover

Grover has the GPT-2 architecture and BPE vocabulary and is trained on **RealNews**, a 120GB news corpus built from Common Crawl. During training the metadata fields (domain, date, authors, headline, body) are split at random, which lets any field be produced conditioned on the rest. Discriminators (Grover, GPT-2, BERT, FastText) learn to tell human news from machine news using Grover-Mega output at various nucleus p values, in an unpaired setting (label a single article) or a paired one (one real and one machine article sharing metadata, where the machine article must receive the higher Machine probability).

## Results (self-reported)

- **Human study (MTurk, five propaganda sites, Grover-Mega at p=0.96):** rewriting propaganda with Grover lifts its overall trustworthiness rating on a 3-point scale, from 2.19 to 2.42.
- **Detection:** Used as a discriminator, Grover spots Grover-Mega text with roughly 92% accuracy when it sees 5,000 machine examples; the best of the other pretrained discriminators reach 73%, as the text states (hardest top-p setting, April 2019 articles, real news without limit). Grover-Mega judging its own output reaches 91.6% in the unpaired setting.
- Discrimination is hardest for nucleus p of roughly 0.94 to 0.98, depending on the discriminator. With a bigger generator, the Grover discriminator falls under 81% accuracy, whereas a bigger discriminator goes past 98%.
- **Weak supervision:** when Grover-Large generations serve as weak supervision, 16 to 256 Grover-Mega examples produce about 78%, versus about 50% with none, and accuracy converges at about 92%.
- On April 2019 article bodies Grover-Mega has a conditional perplexity of 8.7, and metadata conditioning takes 0.6 to 0.9 points off that. Training Grover-Mega cost about $25k (256 TPU v3 cores for two weeks at $0.30 per core-hour).
- The authors tie detection to **exposure bias** (training sees only human text, never the model's own generations) and give the probability that a human-written article has all tokens in the top-p% of the distribution as p^n for length n (Section 6).

## Limits

- Only whole articles generated from minimal context; no comments, edits to real articles, images or video.
- Discriminators use distributional features, not evidence, and do no fact checking. The detection result depends on the verifier having seen output from the same generator family, and a generator trained on its own predictions, or one sampling tokens in a different order, may get past it.
- Adversarial Filtering was ineffective on 1024-token sequences, and the discriminator saw at most 1024 BPE tokens; longer text made detection easier.
- Single runs without seeds or intervals. The human raters assessed style and plausibility rather than truth; since real news can be flagged wrongly and social biases remain, people must stay involved.
