# Demystifying Agentic RL (DemyAgent-4B)

Yu et al. (NUS, UIUC, Princeton, October 2025) study what matters in agentic RL with a single code-interpreter tool. Their model, DemyAgent-4B, is Qwen3-4B with SFT on real tool trajectories followed by GRPO-TCR.

## Findings

- **Real trajectories beat stitched synthetic ones for the SFT start.** The real set holds 3k end-to-end trajectories generated by teacher Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B, built from three 1k pools: LeetCode, ReTool (filtered with ReasonFlux-PRM) and s1. On Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507, AIME24 average@32 rose from 4.38% to 33.23% and AIME25 from 3.65% to 29.79% compared with ReTool-style synthetic data. The 7B gain was smaller (AIME24 6.77 to 17.91).
- **GRPO-TCR** combines three parts: a token-level loss, overlong reward shaping, and clip-higher with the upper bound eps_high set to 0.28 and the lower eps_low to 0.20. The GRPO-T baseline (token-level loss, eps 0.2, outcome plus tool reward, KL beta 0.001) reached a best average@32 of 54.7% on AIME24 and 40.93% on AIME25. On Qwen3-4B, TCR matched that after 100 steps, a quarter of the compute, and went on to 70.93% and 68.13% by step 450.
- **Token-level loss wins over sequence-level only on the stronger model** (on Qwen3-4B the margin is 3.95 points on AIME24 and 3.86 on AIME25; on Qwen2.5-7B the two are about equal), though the sequence-level run used very different clip values.
- **Diverse data raises entropy and speeds learning.** The 30k set mixes 17k DAPO-Math, 3k MegaScience science problems, and math (4,902) and code (3,586) items from Skywork-or1. It crossed 50% average@32 on AIME25 after 150 steps; DAPO-Math-17k alone needed 220.
- **Model-aware filtering rescues a weak model.** With 8 rollouts per problem, 0% and 100% accuracy problems are dropped, and easy (at least 0.75) and hard (at most 0.25) buckets are matched to the model's difficulty histogram; the Qwen2.5-7B run, whose reward had stayed near zero on the full 30k set, is shown to recover only as curves.
- **Clip bound.** With eps_high at 0.315 the result that took 100 steps at 0.28 arrived by step 60, which is 40% faster; at 0.35 Qwen3-4B did worse. Weaker models want larger bounds. The authors' takeaway is that agentic RL needs balanced policy entropy.
- **pass@k and average@k together.** With the TCR or SCR recipes both rise by over 10% on AIME24/25, unlike conventional RL; GRPO-T showed the usual trade-off and early entropy collapse.
- **Reasoning mode.** Deliberative runs (longer thinking, fewer tool calls) reach over 70% tool success, which the paper reports as better than reactive runs; this is a correlation across runs. Long-CoT models such as Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507 learn to steer clear of the tool, with calls falling to zero. Running SFT on the 3k tool trajectories brings tool use back, but the outcome only matches instruct-based starts, which is why the authors favour those.

Reward (Eq. 8): +1+0.1n if the answer matches, -1+0.1n otherwise, where 0.1n is the tool bonus; the paper says the bonus is capped to prevent tool abuse, yet the printed formula has no cap. Overlong shaping costs nothing inside the budget, ramps linearly over the final L_cache tokens, and is -1 past L_max.

## Reported results (self-reported)

Setup: code interpreter, temperature 1.0, top_p 0.6, 16,384 max response, 32 samples per problem; baseline numbers appear copied from other papers. DemyAgent-4B scores AIME24 72.6, AIME25 70.0, GPQA-Diamond 58.5 and LiveCodeBench-v6 26.8, against ReTool-32B at 72.5 / 54.3 on the first two. rStar2-Agent-14B, at 80.6 on AIME24, 69.8 on AIME25 and 60.9 on GPQA, beats it on AIME24 and GPQA. Training: VeRL, 8 A100-80G; SFT 5 epochs at lr 5e-5; RL 3 epochs at lr 1e-6.

## Limits

- Only 4B and 7B models and one static tool (the code interpreter); multi-tool and search settings untested; evaluation is math, science and code (AIME, GPQA, LiveCodeBench), with no function-calling or web-agent test.
- No seeds or variance; many results are single-run curves. Clip-higher and overlong shaping are bundled in TCR; the comparison of loss granularity is confounded because clip values differ, and the eps_high sweep covers only three values.
- Because the SFT teacher is Qwen3-Coder-30B, the real-versus-synthetic gap could partly come from distilling a stronger model. DAPO-Math is in the RL set while AIME is the evaluation, and contamination goes undiscussed.
- Text and table disagree on AIME25 in Table 1 (29.97% / 45.22% in the text, 29.79% / 45.82% in the table).
