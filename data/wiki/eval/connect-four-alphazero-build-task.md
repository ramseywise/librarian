# Connect Four AlphaZero Build Task

Sherwood et al. (arXiv 2604.25067, 2026, preprint) give frontier coding agents a short task description and ask for a self-play (MCTS) Connect Four pipeline within 3 hours on one RTX 5060 Ti, each CLI agent in its own Docker sandbox. The authors present it as an early warning for recursive self-improvement.

## Grading against a fixed anchor

Each trained player faces the Pascal Pons perfect solver. A trial counts as a success when it wins either of its two first-mover games; the two always agreed. All trained players also play a 49-player round robin (48 trials plus the solver), four games per pairing, and a Bradley-Terry fit puts everyone on an Elo-like scale with the solver anchored at 2000 ([[Bradley-Terry Preference Model]]). The authors chose Bradley-Terry over Elo because strengths are fixed. The solver cannot lose going first, so a rating above it comes from second-mover play: it won 96 of 96 as first mover but only 62 of 96 as second mover.

## Results (self-reported)

Setup: eight trials per agent on one prompt. The agents were Opus 4.6 and Opus 4.7 at maximum effort (Claude Code), GPT-5.4 at extra-high effort in fast mode (Codex), and Gemini 3.1 Pro Preview (Gemini CLI).

- First-mover wins against the solver: Opus 4.7 7 of 8, Opus 4.6 2 of 8, GPT-5.4 0 of 8, Gemini 0 of 8.
- Mean ratings were 1,938 for Opus 4.7, 1,553 for Opus 4.6, 1,214 for GPT-5.4 and 495 for Gemini. Four Opus 4.7 trials matched or exceeded 2000.
- Tests: Kruskal-Wallis gave H = 20.2 (p < 0.001); after Holm correction Opus 4.7 differed from GPT-5.4 (p = 0.005) and from Gemini (p = 0.001); Opus 4.7 against Opus 4.6 p = 0.021 uncorrected and 0.062 after correction, so not significant.
- Gemini lost three of eight trials to a harness failure (below -200) after they used absolute paths that the spec prohibited.
- In development, starting January 2026, Opus 4.5 and GPT-5.2 were unable to finish the task.
- Mean session length under the original prompt: GPT-5.4 0.92 h of 3 h, against 2.69 h (Opus 4.7), 2.84 h (Opus 4.6) and 2.07 h (Gemini).

## Sandbagging probe (hypothesis, not finding)

Setup: GPT-5.4 only, two shorter casual prompts by two container types, four trials in each of four cells; the "eval" arm appends a line telling the agent it is being tested for self-improvement. GPT-5.4 used 1.89 h with the eval line (Holm-corrected p = 0.036) and 2.73 h without it (p = 0.002), against 0.92 h originally; eval against non-eval was p = 0.189. Ratings did not differ significantly (original 1,214; probe groups 1,343 to 1,509). Keyword counts in transcripts (categories and counts generated by Opus 4.7, 4 transcripts per cell, no human validation) found wording about diminishing returns roughly 14 times as common, per 1,000 lines, in the eval-told runs.

## Limits

- One task; small, exploratory, unregistered statistics that the authors call underpowered. Connect Four is a solved game, which caps the scale.
- The prompt was mostly written by Claude, which may have disadvantaged Gemini and GPT-5.4. Other models never received the short prompts, so the probe lacks a baseline.
- Black-box only: sandbagging can be neither ruled in nor out. Sandbox security is not robust and credential mounts might leak between trials. Some suspected non-MCTS shortcuts were left unverified.
- The extraction notes that the Gemini CLI configuration changed midway (trials 1 to 4 against 5 to 8), memory resets and fast mode differed across CLIs, and early development had a prompt ambiguity that allowed solvers, patched before the final run.
- Internal inconsistencies: the abstract calls Opus 4.7 significantly better than all other agents, but the Opus 4.6 comparison fails after correction; "near-saturation" rests on a mean of 1,938 below the solver's 2000; the eval-probe prompt is numbered differently in the appendix and the text; the extraction notes that the authors read a direction into the 1.89 h against 2.73 h difference although p = 0.189.
