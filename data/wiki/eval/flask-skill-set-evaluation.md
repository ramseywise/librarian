# FLASK Skill-Set Evaluation

FLASK (Ye et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv 2307.10928) is a benchmark and grading protocol that breaks
"alignment" into **12 skills in four abilities**: Logical Thinking (logical correctness,
robustness, efficiency), Background Knowledge (factuality, commonsense), Problem Handling
(comprehension, insightfulness, completeness, metacognition) and User Alignment (conciseness,
readability, harmlessness). Each skill has its own 1-5 rubric. The set holds 1,740 instances
drawn from 122 datasets, and GPT-4 labels each instance with its top three relevant skills, one
of 10 domains and a difficulty from 1 to 5. A grader gives feedback first and then a score for
each relevant skill. FLASK-Hard (89 instances from 25 datasets) swaps the generic rubric for an
instance-specific checklist: GPT-4 drafts up to five sub-questions per instance, the authors
prune them, and each is scored 1-5.

## Results (self-reported)

- **Rubric against single score.** The sample was 200 instances, each answered by four models: GPT-3.5, Bard, Vicuna-13B and
  Alpaca-13B, 3 of 10 labelers per instance, gpt-4-0613 as grader: skill-specific rubrics gave
  Spearman 0.680, Kendall 0.541 and Pearson 0.732 against 0.641, 0.495 and 0.673 for a
  reference-guided skill-agnostic prompt. ROUGE-L scored 0.333. With GPT-3.5 as grader the
  Spearman figures were 0.424 and 0.360 (Pearson flat at 0.449 against 0.450); with Claude, 0.432
  and 0.352.
- **Ablation** (GPT-4 grader, same set): Spearman 0.516 without the reference answer, 0.634
  without the rationale, 0.646 without the rubric.
- **Agreement.** Krippendorff's alpha was 0.488 between humans, 0.835 for GPT-4 across 3 runs at
  temperature 1.0, and 0.471 across three grader models. Readability had the lowest human
  agreement (0.089) and correctness the highest (0.730).
- **Padding robustness.** When GPT-3.5 answers on FLASK-Hard were rewritten to be more verbose,
  the same score was kept on 43.82% of items under a skill-agnostic rubric, 52.99% under skill-specific
  and 55.78% under instance-specific.
- **Length.** Response length correlated 0.737 with completeness and 0.763 with insightfulness,
  against 0.147 with logical correctness.
- **Models.** On Logical Robustness, Vicuna scored 2.26, Tulu-65B 2.66 and LLaMA2-70B 2.59
  against 3.94 for GPT-3.5 and 4.22 for GPT-4, while Readability was 4.65 against 4.86. GPT-4's
  Logical Correctness fell from 4.22 to 3.00 on FLASK-Hard.
- **Label validation.** Humans accepted 95.22% of skill labels and 81.32% of domain labels on 200
  instances; difficulty correlated 0.774 (Pearson) with human ratings.

## Limits

The human study covers 200 instances, 4 models and 10 labelers from one university, and the
authors name central-tendency bias, fatigue, a grader preference for longer answers and its own
style, and English single-turn zero-shot scope. The Hard set is small enough that outliers matter.
The instance-specific checklists were never validated by humans; their only evidence is the
padding test. Table 1 has no significance tests, and the GPT-4 gain is roughly 0.04 Spearman.
GPT-4 acts as grader and is also graded, missing references were generated by GPT-4, and domain
labels are wrong about one time in five. The paper gives both 1,740 and 1,700 instances. Models
are 2023 versions, so the open-versus-proprietary gap is a snapshot.
