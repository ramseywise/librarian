# LLM Trustworthiness Taxonomy

Liu and colleagues (2023 preprint) propose seven dimensions for judging whether an LLM is aligned, with 29 sub-categories in all, and test eight of the sub-categories. The authors concede that the seven dimensions leave parts of trustworthiness uncovered. Their one-line glosses:

- **Reliability**: right, honest and stable answers, expressed with fitting confidence.
- **Safety**: avoiding unsafe or illegal output and leaks of private information.
- **Fairness**: avoiding bias and disparate performance.
- **Resistance to Misuse**: prohibiting malicious use.
- **Explainability and Reasoning**: explaining outputs and reasoning correctly.
- **Social Norm**: reflecting widely shared human values.
- **Robustness**: resilience against adversarial attacks and distribution shift.

## Measurement study (self-reported)

Test prompts were mostly generated by text-davinci-003 from keywords, the target model answered, and gpt-4 judged the answers. Six models were reached through APIs between May and July 2023. Most scores appear only in bar charts, so the findings below are directional:

- Hallucination (multiple choice, built from HaluEval questions): the unaligned davinci and OPT-1.3B make up more answers, and gpt-4 makes up the fewest.
- Safety: 984 unsafe prompts, generated with a "Write a BAD question that involves KEYWORD" template; gpt-3.5-turbo and gpt-4 refuse close to 100% of them. Gender-stereotype refusal on 361 multiple-choice questions is much lower, under 60% even for gpt-4.
- Misuse: on prompts about propaganda against people, terrorism advocacy and cyberattacks the aligned ChatGPT and GPT-4 refuse far more, but for extreme political propaganda the trend reverses (more aligned, more compliant).
- Copyright: with 1,000 sentence starts from one novel, davinci reproduced text more than 90% similar about 20% of the time and gpt-4 18% of the time; no clear link to alignment.
- Causality: gpt-4 stays under 70% on the necessary-cause question, on events it generated itself.
- Robustness: typos in the prompt made all models lose consistency; ChatGPT and GPT-4 mostly answered No.
- Calibration: verbalised confidence compared with internal confidence by Earth mover distance; text-davinci-003 was the most consistent. This measures agreement, not correctness.
- Alignment data: gpt-4 preferred a GPT-2 fine-tuned on the generated safety data over vanilla GPT-2 in 71% of 100 test prompts (13% the other way), and PPO over SFT in 38% against 32% (self-reported). LLaMA-7B fine-tuned the same way was also preferred over the base model.

## Limits

- Only 8 of 29 sub-categories are measured; Bard and Claude were not tested.
- Open questions were turned into multiple choice and gpt-4 was treated as a ground-truth judge; a human audit is mentioned but not reported with numbers.
- The study reports no seeds, no confidence intervals and no rate of agreement with the judge; the APIs it called change over time.
- Causal ground truth comes from gpt-4's own generation and may be wrong.
- The calibration check does not measure correctness, and ChatGPT and GPT-4 internal confidence was approximated with 20 samples because logits were unavailable.
