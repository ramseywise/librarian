# TIR-Judge

Xu et al. (arXiv 2510.23038, 2025, preprint) train a judge that produces and executes Python while it judges, so counting, constraint checks, arithmetic and test runs come out exact. It is also trained to leave the tool unused on prompt types where code adds nothing, namely safety and general helpfulness. The authors' motivation is that most judges rely on text-only reasoning, which limits verification of complex constraints and exact computation.

## Variants

- **TIR-Judge-Zero:** no distillation; a loop of reinforcement learning, rejection sampling, supervised fine-tuning, then reinforcement learning again.
- **TIR-Judge-Distill:** supervised fine-tuning on roughly 10k tool-use trajectories from Gemini-2.5-Flash, followed by reinforcement learning.

Both use Qwen3-8B or Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 backbones and about 26k training pairs (26,361). The judge does not see a reference answer at evaluation time.

## Results (self-reported)

- **Headline.** The abstract claims gains over strong reasoning-based judges of as large as 6.4% pointwise and 7.7% pairwise, over seven benchmarks; see Limits on the unit.
- **PPE Correctness.** It beats similar-size baselines by 4.8 to 9.9% pointwise and 4.5 to 8.8% pairwise, and equals RRM-32B on PPE and RewardBench while using between one quarter and one eighth of its parameters.
- **RewardBench2, listwise best-of-4, 5 subsets.** Zero 8B averaged 73.4; the other averages were Claude-Opus-4 76.5, Gemini-2.5-flash-Preview 75.9, Distill 8B 71.6, then GPT-4.1 at 69.7, Claude-Sonnet-4 at 69.5 and GPT-4o at 64.9. Distill 8B got 58.1 on the IF subset against Opus-4's 41.9, while on Fact Opus-4 scored 82.7 and Zero 8B 64.8.
- **Tool alone.** Prompting Qwen3 with the same code tool gave negligible (under 1%) or negative gains; Qwen3-8B-Tool averaged 59.4 pointwise against 64.9 for plain Qwen3-8B. The authors conclude that reinforcement learning is what unlocks tool use.
- **Best-of-N.** On AIME and BigCodeBench, Zero gained 3.9 to 6.7% absolute over RRM-7B (16 samples per problem, 16k token limit). On BigCodeBench Deepseek-Coder (1,139 problems), 285 pass at Pass@1, 328 with majority vote over 16, and 435 with the 4B pointwise Zero judge.
- **Cost, 8xH100.** Zero took 29.0 hours at about $690; Distill took 11.0 hours at about $340, split into $210 of compute and $130 for the teacher's API.

## Limits

The only tool is code execution, and the gains sit in verifiable tasks (instruction following, math, code); on Chat and Safety in RM-Bench, text-only judges scored slightly higher. Gemini-2.5-Flash sometimes did worse with tools, which the extraction attributes to a cap on turns and tool calls that ran away (appendix B). The evaluation is accuracy alone: bias, position bias and calibration are not assessed, and pairwise tests use one random ordering. Part of the training data is synthetic pairs generated by Qwen3 and Gemma models, labelled using the benchmark's ground-truth reward model, with eight-gram decontamination applied. Backbones are Qwen3 4B and 8B only, some baseline numbers come from other papers, and on many benchmarks several baselines above 10B parameters still score higher. The authors make no claim a way to tell when a tool is needed beyond a format heuristic that skips it for safety and helpfulness. One run, no variance.

Internal inconsistencies: the paper does not say whether the 6.4% and 7.7% are absolute points or relative. Its introduction puts Zero 1.2% ahead of Distill at 4B, but Table 1's 4B pairwise averages are 73.7 and 70.5, which do not match, and at 8B pairwise Distill is ahead (72.6 against 72.0). The extraction advises checking the 1.2% before citing it.
