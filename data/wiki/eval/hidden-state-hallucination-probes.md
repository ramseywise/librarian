# Hidden-State Hallucination Probes

Both papers ask whether a model's hidden activations carry a signal about whether its output is right. Both are white-box: the probe reads the model's internal states.

## SAPLMA

Azaria and Mitchell (Findings of EMNLP 2023) hypothesise that the truth of a statement is represented in, and can be pulled out of, an LLM's internal state. **SAPLMA** trains a small feedforward classifier on the activations of one hidden layer of a frozen model and outputs the probability the statement is true. They release a 6,084-sentence true-false dataset over six topics, train on five and test on the held-out sixth, and also test on 245 statements generated by OPT-6.7b.

- On held-out topics SAPLMA beat BERT and few-shot baselines: best at the 20th layer for OPT-6.7b and the 16th (middle) layer for LLAMA2-7b, mean of three runs (self-reported). Three-shot prompting of OPT-6.7b was near chance and zero-shot prompting failed.
- On OPT-6.7b's own generated statements at the 0.5 threshold, the best layer (28th) was only modestly above chance; with a threshold tuned on a 30% validation split it improved on the remaining 70%. At 0.5 the classifier labelled about 70% of generated sentences true, which the authors read as plausible sentences being called true. GPT-4 labelled the same statements far more accurately.
- Training on randomly permuted labels gave 62.5% training accuracy, which the authors use to argue the classifier cannot just overfit.
- Sentence probability was unreliable as a truth signal in a 14-statement hand-made check, since it depends on syntax and length.

## WACK

Simhi et al. (arXiv 2410.22071) separate two kinds of hallucination: **HK-**, where the model lacks the knowledge, and **HK+**, where it produces correct answers under some prompts yet wrong ones under similar prompts. **WACK** builds a model-specific dataset: a closed-book question is HK- if the model never produces the gold answer in 6 generations under a 3-shot prompt; otherwise it is high-knowledge, and is re-asked under four hallucination-inducing settings (Truthful, Persona, Alice-Bob, Snowballing) and labelled HK+ if the answer turns wrong. Linear SVM probes on hidden states then classify the labels, and are compared with generic datasets built from questions, gold answers and Mistral-written wrong answers.

- HK+ made up 4 to 24 percent of high-knowledge cases across settings (TriviaQA and Natural Questions; Mistral-7B-v0.3, Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B), with Snowballing highest on Gemma with Natural Questions; even the Truthful setting produced HK+ on TriviaQA for Gemma.
- A prompt prefix encouraging factuality recovered far more HK+ than HK- cases; for Gemma-2-9B on TriviaQA in the Alice-Bob setting the success rate was 9.0 to 23.4 for HK+ against 1.8 to 2.6 for HK-. The authors attribute the nonzero HK- rate to mislabelling.
- A three-way probe (TriviaQA, three models, three seeds) reached 60 to 70% at the top layer for Snowballing and about 50% for the other settings, against 33% chance.
- HK+ examples overlapped little between models (Jaccard 0.1 to 0.36), while high-knowledge sets overlapped about 0.8 on TriviaQA and 0.6 on Natural Questions. Model-specific probes detected HK+ at 60 to 70% at some layers (Mistral, Snowballing), where generic-dataset detectors were near or below random. A probe trained on Snowballing and tested on Alice-Bob lost about 5% at layer 15.

All figures above are self-reported.

## Limits

SAPLMA was tested only in English on OPT-6.7b and LLAMA2-7b. Statements are classified in isolation, accuracy on generated statements was about 0.64 to 0.71 and their truth labels are partly subjective, the best layer differs by model and test set without explanation, and output is binary only, with no "unsure". The threshold for the generated-statement result was tuned on 30% of that test set. WACK used three models, four synthetic settings, short-answer closed-book QA and exact-match knowledge labels; the middle of the knowledge spectrum (about 5% of questions) was excluded. Probe accuracy is moderate and the authors say more research is needed. HK+ is defined by prompt perturbation, so a question "known" under a 3-shot prompt may reflect prompt sensitivity, not stored knowledge. Mitigation was tried with one prefix and one paraphrase.
