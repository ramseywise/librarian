# GraphRAG Local-to-Global Community Summaries

Edge et al. (preprint, 2024). The question is how to answer global sensemaking questions, which need understanding of a whole corpus and which vector RAG (retrieval by embedding similarity) is not built for.

## Mechanism

- Source text is cut into 600-token chunks; an LLM pulls entities, relationships and claims out of each, and a graph is built from them.
- The graph is partitioned hierarchically with Leiden community detection, and an LLM writes a report for each community, giving summaries at several levels (the paper compares levels C0 to C3).
- At query time the community summaries are shuffled into batches, each batch yields partial answers by map, and the partial answers are combined by reduce.
- Comparison conditions: each graph level, vector RAG (SS), and a map-reduce over raw source text that uses no graph (TS), judged head to head.

## Results (self-reported)

Setup for all results unless noted: GPT-4-turbo; for each dataset (podcast transcripts of about 1M tokens, news articles of about 1.7M tokens) 125 questions generated by an LLM; an LLM judge compares systems pairwise, averaging 5 replicates; context window 8k (the vector baseline's best across 8k to 64k). The extraction lost the win-rate numbers, so only the direction is recorded.

- Global methods beat vector RAG on comprehensiveness and diversity on both datasets. Vector RAG won on directness, a control criterion, against every graph level; empowerment was mixed and the lowest level lost to vector RAG on the podcast set.
- The graph-free text map-reduce matched or beat the graph levels on several comparisons, with only a small graph gain at one level on the podcast set. The highest summary level used fewer context tokens than the text map-reduce.
- A second experiment extracted claims with Claimify. Vector RAG gave fewer claims per answer than all global conditions, and there was no significant difference among global conditions or against the text map-reduce on claim measures. The LLM judge agreed with the claim-based winner on comprehensiveness in the non-tie cases, which are 33% of comparisons (39% for diversity).
- Indexing cost: graphs of 8,564 nodes and 20,691 edges (podcast) and 15,754 nodes and 19,520 edges (news), built on a 16 GB Intel Xeon VM with GPT-4-turbo.

## Limits

- Two corpora of about 1M tokens each, so generalisation is unknown; the authors note fabrication rates were not compared.
- LLM judge only, with LLM-generated questions and no human judges or ground truth. Decoding temperature, judge model identity, seeds and iteration limits are not stated.
- Directness favours vector RAG. The extraction notes the graph-free baseline matches or beats graph levels in several cells.
