# CAMEL Role-Playing Inception Prompting

CAMEL (2023) proposes *role-playing*: two agents, an AI user and an AI assistant, each handed a role and a task, talk in turns of instruction and solution until the task is finished. A **task specifier** agent first turns a human idea plus the two roles into a concrete task. **Inception prompting** means all prompt engineering happens at the start (task specifier, assistant system and user system prompts), after which the agents prompt each other in a loop. Both agents are gpt-3.5-turbo. A conversation ends if the user agent outputs the token `<CAMEL_TASK_DONE>`, or if some termination rule fires, and a 40-message cap bounds the cost. The recorded conversations make up the AI Society and Code datasets, alongside Math and Science QA sets generated by GPT-4; LLaMA-7B is fine-tuned on these.

## Failure modes

The paper names four: **role flipping**, **the assistant repeating the instruction**, **flake replies** (the assistant says "I will..." and promises action without taking it) and an **infinite loop of messages**. Counts appear only in figures: most AI Society chats end on the Assistant Instruct flag, whereas in Code the leading cause of termination is the token limit.

## Results (self-reported)

- On AI Society, with 100 random tasks and each CAMEL conversation first summarised by GPT4, 453 human responses favour CAMEL over single-shot gpt-3.5-turbo (13.3% draws, 10.4% wins for gpt-3.5-turbo). A GPT4 judge also favours CAMEL on AI Society (23.0% wins for gpt-3.5-turbo, 4.0% draws) and on Code (24.0% and 0.0%), and when the opponent is gpt-3.5-turbo using zero-shot CoT (28.0% and 4.0%).
- Taking the communication-protocol and alignment lines out of the inception prompt hurts, with the original prompt winning 75.0% to 25.0%.
- CAMEL-7B beats LLaMA-7B (10.5) and Vicuna-7B (11.0) on HumanEval pass@1, with gpt-3.5-turbo at 69.4.
- lm-evaluation-harness average: CAMEL 13B 58.0 against 56.1 for LLaMA 13B; a 33B variant reaches 64 against 64.2 for LLaMA 65B.

## Limits

- The authors concede that GPT4 and human judges may favour longer answers. CAMEL conversations take far more calls and tokens than the single-shot baseline, and that cost is not compared.
- GPT4 summarises CAMEL solutions before they are judged, so the comparison is not one output against another, and human evaluation covers AI Society alone.
- No seeds, repeats, intervals or decoding settings; only two agents are studied.
- The fine-tuning gains rest on GPT-generated data, which is licensed for research use only; the malicious-use risk is shown by one example with no quantified safety result.
- The extraction notes the Table 2 winner tables were scrambled in its cache.
