- Frame an analytical request as a decision, metric, baseline, and validation plan.

- Segment customers responsibly, validate later usefulness, and distinguish targeting from persuadability.
- Use analytical SQL to reconcile changes in a business total and explain their drivers.
- Model time to an event, treating customers who have not left yet as censored (Survival and event history).
- Model customers as states and sequences, and check a transition matrix on a holdout before using it to forecast (States and sequences).
- Separate attribution from incrementality when you allocate a marketing budget (Marketing measurement).
- Turn a forecast's error distribution into stock and staffing decisions (Operations and supply chain).
- Design a survey, weight it to known totals, and report what its margin of error omits (Surveys and sampling design).
